## Gate Two: The Moat — What Keeps Competitors Out for Ten Years?

The second gate is the moat. I have said that the most important thing to look for in a business is a durable competitive advantage — a moat that protects it from competition. I have owned businesses with incredible moats: Coca-Cola (brand), American Express (network effect), GEICO (cost advantage), Moody’s (switching costs). These are businesses that can fend off competitors for decades.

Let me apply the four types of moats to Alight.

**Cost Advantage:** Does Alight have a structural cost advantage over its competitors? No. The HR software industry is not like GEICO, where we have the lowest expense ratio in auto insurance. Alight is a service business. It has to hire people, build software, and maintain data centers. Its costs are roughly the same as its competitors. There is no magic formula that allows Alight to produce its product cheaper than Workday or ADP.

**Network Effect:** Does Alight get more valuable the more people use it? No. A payroll system does not become more valuable because more companies use it. It is not a social network. It is not a payment network like American Express or Visa. There is no network effect here. Each customer is a separate instance, a separate contract, a separate implementation. There is no viral growth.

**High Switching Costs:** This is the most promising angle, but it is not strong enough. Yes, it is painful for a large company to switch payroll and benefits providers. The implementation process takes months, sometimes years. There are data migrations, employee training, regulatory compliance issues. So there is some stickiness. But is it enough to keep competitors out for ten years? No. Companies switch all the time. They switch because of price, because of features, because of customer service. The switching cost is real, but it is not a moat. It is a speed bump.

**Intangible Assets:** Does Alight have a brand that allows it to charge a premium? No. When is the last time you heard a CEO say, “We use Alight because it is the best brand in HR software”? Never. Alight is not Coca-Cola. It is not See’s Candy. It is a commodity service sold on price and features. There is no pricing power.

I have a simple test for a moat. I ask myself: “If I had a billion dollars and I wanted to compete with this business, could I do it?” For Coca-Cola, the answer is no.
