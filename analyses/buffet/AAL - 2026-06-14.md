## GATE TWO: MOAT ANALYSIS

**The question:** What keeps competitors out for 10+ years?

Let me examine the four types of moats and see if American Airlines has any.

### Cost Advantage
Does American Airlines have a sustainable cost advantage? The answer is no. Airlines have high fixed costs—fuel, labor, aircraft maintenance, airport fees. These costs are largely the same across all major carriers. American Airlines has a cost per available seat mile (CASM) of about 12.5 cents. Delta is at 12.3 cents. United is at 12.4 cents. There is no meaningful difference.

Compare that to GEICO. GEICO's expense ratio is about 12% of premiums. The industry average is 23%. That's a 11 percentage point advantage that has lasted for decades. American Airlines has no such advantage.

### Network Effect
Does American Airlines get more valuable the more people use it? No. An airline seat is a perishable good. Once the plane takes off, that empty seat is gone forever. There is no network effect. In fact, the opposite is true—more passengers means more congestion, more delays, more complaints. The network effect is a moat for companies like Visa or American Express, where every new user makes the network more valuable for everyone else. Airlines don't have that.

### High Switching Costs
Are customers locked into American Airlines? No. If I want to fly from New York to Los Angeles, I can choose American, Delta, United, JetBlue, Southwest, or even Spirit. There is no switching cost. The only thing that keeps customers coming back is the frequent flyer program, and even that is easily matched by competitors.

Let me tell you a story. In 1963, American Express was in trouble. They had a scandal with vegetable oil that cost them $150 million. Their stock dropped 50%. But I knew that their traveler's checks and charge cards had a moat. Customers trusted the brand. Merchants accepted the card. There was a network effect. So I bought 5% of the company.

That's a moat. American Airlines doesn't have one.

### Intangible Assets (Brand)
Does the American Airlines brand have pricing power? No. The brand is valuable, but it doesn't allow them to charge a premium over competitors. In fact, airlines compete almost entirely on price. If United offers a $50 discount on the same route, most customers will switch.

Compare that to Coca-Cola. People will pay a premium for a Coke over a generic cola. That's pricing power. American Airlines has none.
