# AMD — LYNCH Analysis — 2026-06-14\n\n**Subject: My Take on AMD – A Fast Grower That’s Testing Every Rule I’ve Ever Written**

**From:** Peter Lynch

**To:** My Loyal Readers

You know, I’ve been getting a lot of mail lately. People asking me about the hot stocks, the ones that are up 50% in a year, the ones that are on every financial news channel. And the name that keeps popping up is Advanced Micro Devices. AMD. The ticker is AMD. And I have to tell you, when I look at this company, my brain starts firing on all cylinders, but my gut is sending up a few warning flares.

Let’s get the numbers on the table. Today, June 14th, 2026, AMD is trading at **$511.57**. It’s up **4.73%** today. The market cap is a staggering **$834 billion**. That’s not a typo. Eight hundred and thirty-four billion dollars. The P/E ratio is **169**. One hundred and sixty-nine. The earnings per share is just **$3.02**.

Now, you might look at that P/E and think, "Peter, you’re a value guy. You wrote *One Up On Wall Street*. You’d run screaming from a stock with a 169 P/E." And you’d be right, in a way. But I’m not a "value" guy in the way most people think. I’m a GARP guy. Growth at a Reasonable Price. And the key word there is *reasonable*. A P/E of 169 is not reasonable for a slow grower. It’s not even reasonable for a stalwart. But for a fast grower? A company that’s growing earnings at 95% a quarter? That’s where it gets interesting.

Let’s start with the most important tool in my toolbox: the **Six Category System**. We have to figure out what kind of animal we’re dealing with. Is this a slow grower, a stalwart, a fast grower, a cyclical, a turnaround, or an asset play?

**Category Identification: Fast Grower (The Only Logical Home)**

This is a fast grower. No question. The quarterly EPS growth is **95.1%**. That’s a number you don’t see very often. I’ve managed money for 13 years at Magellan, and a 95% growth rate is the kind of thing that gets your heart pumping. But here’s the thing about fast growers: they are the most dangerous category of all if you buy at the wrong price.

Think of it like a restaurant. McDonald’s was a fast grower in the 1960s and 70s. They were opening new stores, selling more burgers, and the stock went up 100-fold. But if you bought McDonald’s at the peak of its growth in 1972, when everyone was talking about it, you would have lost 60% of your money in the next two years, even though the company was still doing fine. The stock got ahead of itself.

That’s the risk with AMD today. The story is fantastic. They make the best chips for data centers, for AI, for gaming, for PCs. They’re taking market share from Intel. They have a clear roadmap. The CEO, Lisa Su, is a legend. But the price? The price is everything.

**The "Two-Minute Story" Test**

I have a rule. You should be able to explain why you own a stock to a 10-year-old in about two minutes. If you can’t, you don’t understand it well enough to own it.

So, let’s try it with AMD.

"See, Timmy, there’s this company called AMD. They make the brains for computers. Not just your laptop, but the big computers in the cloud that run all your apps, your games, and even the new AI stuff. They make really, really good brains. They’re better than the brains from their big rival, Intel. So, more and more companies are buying AMD brains. And every time a company buys one, AMD makes money. And because they’re so good, they can charge a lot for them."

That’s it. That’s the story. It’s simple. It’s powerful. It passes the test.

**The PEG Ratio: The Most Important Number**

Now, let’s get into the math. The PEG ratio is the most important metric for a fast grower. It’s the P/E divided by the growth rate. You want a PEG under 1.5. Ideally, under 1.0.

AMD’s P/E is **169.39**. The growth rate is **95.1%** (quarterly). Let’s do the math.

PEG = 169.39 / 95.1 = **1.78**

That’s a **1.78 PEG**. It’s above 1.5. It’s not a screaming buy. It’s not a "no-brainer." But it’s also not a disaster. If the growth rate was 50%, the PEG would be 3.4, and I’d say "run away." But 95% growth is so powerful that even a high P/E can be justified, for a while.

But here’s the catch. That growth rate is quarterly. It might be a one-time thing. It might be because of a massive AI chip order. It might be because of a product cycle. You have to ask the question I always ask for fast growers: **"How can growth continue?"**

**The "How Can Growth Continue?" Test**

For a fast grower, you need a story that’s not just about today, but about tomorrow. You need to see the runway.

For AMD, the runway is long. They have three massive engines:

1.  **Data Center:** This is the big one. The cloud providers – Amazon, Microsoft, Google – are spending billions on AI chips. AMD’s MI300 series and future chips are direct competitors to Nvidia. If AMD can take even 20% of that market, it’s a multi-hundred-billion-dollar opportunity.

2.  **PCs and Gaming:** This is the bread and butter. AMD has been gaining share in desktop and laptop CPUs. The gaming console market (PlayStation, Xbox) is all AMD. It’s a stable, recurring revenue stream.

3.  **Embedded:** This is the sleeper. AMD bought Xilinx, which makes chips for everything from cars to industrial equipment. It’s a slower-growing but high-margin business.

The question is: can they keep up the 95% growth? Probably not. No one can. But if they can grow at 30-40% for the next three years, the stock will do very well. If growth slows to 10%, the stock will get crushed.

**The Earnings Test: "Something Dynamic Must Keep Earnings Moving"**

I have another rule. For a stock to go up, something dynamic must be happening to earnings. It’s not enough to say "the company is good." You need a catalyst.

For AMD, the catalyst is clear: **the AI boom**. Every major tech company is building out its AI infrastructure. They need the best chips. AMD is one of the two companies that can provide them. That’s the dynamic.

But I also look at the balance sheet. AMD has very little debt. They have a lot of cash. That’s a great sign. It means they can invest in R&D, buy back stock, or make acquisitions. They’re not at risk of bankruptcy.

Let’s look at the chart, because the price tells a story.

- **Current Price:** $511.57

- **200-day Moving Average:** $253.93

The RSI is around 60. That’s neutral. It’s not overbought (which is above 70), and it’s not oversold (below 30). It suggests the stock has some room to run, but it’s not a screaming bargain.

**The Sell Rules: The Most Important Part of the Analysis**

You know, I’ve spent more time thinking about when to sell than when to buy. Because everyone tells you what to buy. No one tells you when to get out.

For a fast grower like AMD, my sell rules are:

1.  **Growth is slowing.** If AMD’s quarterly growth drops from 95% to 50%, that’s a warning. If it drops to 20%, I’m selling half my position. The market will punish a slowing fast grower ruthlessly.

2.  **New competition appears.** Right now, AMD’s biggest threat is Nvidia. If Nvidia comes out with a chip that is twice as fast and half the price, AMD’s story is broken. Also, watch for new entrants like Intel or even custom chips from Amazon and Google.

3.  **The company starts doing stupid things.** If AMD starts buying back stock at these nosebleed prices or makes a bad acquisition, that’s a red flag.

4.  **The story becomes too popular.** When your taxi driver starts telling you to buy AMD, it’s time to sell. When every financial magazine has AMD on the cover, it’s time to sell. The stock is already priced for perfection. Any disappointment will be crushed.

**The Comparison to My Old Favorites**

Let me give you some examples from my own experience.

- **McDonald’s (MCD):** When I bought McDonald’s, it was a fast grower. They were opening new stores, adding breakfast, adding drive-thrus. The P/E was reasonable, maybe 15-20. The growth was 20% a year. That’s a PEG of 0.75 to 1.0. That’s a buy. AMD has a PEG of 1.78. It’s more expensive than McDonald’s ever was.

- **Service Corporation International (SCI):** This was a classic fast grower. They were buying up funeral homes, consolidating a fragmented industry. The growth was 20% a year. The P/E was 15. The PEG was 0.75. I loved it. AMD is growing faster, but it’s priced much higher.

- **La Quinta Motor Inns:** Another fast grower. They were building motels in underserved markets. 20% unit growth. P/E of 12. PEG of 0.6. A steal. AMD is not a steal. It’s a premium-priced growth stock.

**The Macro Picture: The Tide That Lifts All Boats**

I can’t ignore the macro environment. The market is in a bull market. Interest rates are low. The economy is growing. That’s a tailwind for all stocks, especially tech.

But I worry about the concentration. The top 10 stocks in the S&P 500 are all tech. They make up 35% of the index. That’s a lot of eggs in one basket. If the AI bubble pops, it will take AMD down with it.

I also look at the beta. AMD’s beta is **2.492**. That means if the market goes up 1%, AMD goes up 2.5%. If the market goes down 1%, AMD goes down 2.5%. It’s a high-volatility stock. You need a strong stomach to own it.

**The Final Verdict: A Speculative Buy, Not a Lynch-Style Investment**

So, what’s my conclusion? I’m going to give you a nuanced answer, because that’s what investing is all about.

If you are a long-term investor, looking to hold for 5-10 years, I would be very cautious. The P/E of 169 is a huge risk. You are paying for perfection. If anything goes wrong – if growth slows, if competition emerges, if the market turns – you could lose 50% or more of your money.

But if you are a trader, or an aggressive growth investor, and you understand the risks, AMD could be a great stock. The AI trend is real. The company is excellent. The growth is phenomenal. The PEG of 1.78 is not insane for a company growing at 95%.

My advice? **Start small.** Don’t put 20% of your portfolio in AMD. Put 2-3%. And then watch it like a hawk. If the stock drops 20% on good earnings, that’s a buying opportunity. If it drops 20% on bad earnings, get out.

Remember the story of SmithKline and Tagamet. I bought SmithKline at $7.50. The P/E was 7. The growth was 20%. The PEG was 0.35. It went to $72. That was a 10-bagger. But I bought it when it was cheap. I didn’t buy it when it was at $50 with a P/E of 40.

AMD is a great company. But it’s not a cheap stock. You have to decide if you’re willing to pay up for the growth.

**My Final Thought: The 2-Minute Story Test Revisited**

I’ll leave you with this. The best investments are the ones you can explain to your spouse over dinner. "Honey, I bought AMD because they make the best chips for AI, and AI is the next big thing." That’s a good story.

But the second part of the story is the price. "And I bought it at a price that gives me a good chance of making money."

Can you say that about AMD at $511? I’m not sure. I’d rather wait for a pullback, or for the growth to prove itself over the next few quarters.

**My Rating:** **HOLD / AGGRESSIVE BUY** (only for those who understand the risks)

**Action:** I would not add to a full position. If you don’t own it, I’d wait for a better entry point, maybe around $400 or $350. If you own it, I’d set a stop-loss at 15% below your purchase price.

Remember, the stock market is a marathon, not a sprint. You don’t have to swing at every pitch. Sometimes the best trade is the one you don’t make.

Good luck.
