First thing I do with any stock, before I even look at the numbers, is I ask myself the two-minute story test. Can I explain what this company does to a ten-year-old in under two minutes? With Google, that’s easy. “Google is a giant library that also sells ads, makes phones, runs a video site, and is trying to build a robot brain.” The kid would get it. Let’s start by categorizing this stock. You’ve got your six categories: Slow Grower, Stalwart, Fast Grower, Cyclical, Turnaround, and Asset Play. Where does Google fit? On the surface, it looks like a Fast Grower. Revenue growth has been solid, but we’re talking about a company with a $4.38 trillion market cap. Can a company that big grow at 20% a year? Unlikely. The law of large numbers is a cruel mistress. Google is probably a Stalwart with some Fast Grower characteristics. Think of it like McDonald’s in the 1980s. McDonald’s was already huge, but it still had room to grow internationally and with new products like Chicken McNuggets. Google is similar. It’s a dominant cash cow—search advertising—that throws off billions in profit, and it’s got these moonshot projects like self-driving cars (Waymo) and cloud computing that could become the next big thing. But the core business is mature. The growth is slowing down. That makes it a Stalwart.

Now, the first test for any Stalwart is the PEG ratio. PEG is P/E divided by the growth rate. You want it under 1.5. Under 1.0 is even better. Google’s P/E is 27.48. That’s not cheap, but it’s not crazy either for a company with this kind of moat. What’s the growth rate? Let’s look at the earnings growth. Quarterly EPS growth is 0.812. That’s 81.2% year-over-year. That’s a huge number. But you have to be careful with quarterly numbers. They can be distorted by one-time items, tax benefits, or comparisons to a weak quarter last year. Let’s look at the trailing twelve months. EPS is $13.09. If I assume a more sustainable growth rate—say 15% to 20%—then the PEG ratio is 27.48 / 15 = 1.83, or 27.48 / 20 = 1.37. That’s borderline. The PEG is above 1.5 on the low end, but under 1.5 on the high end. That tells me the stock is not obviously cheap, but it’s not obviously expensive either. It’s in that gray zone where you need to dig deeper.

Let’s talk about the earnings test. “Something dynamic MUST keep earnings moving.” That’s the rule. For Google, the dynamic force is digital advertising and cloud computing. Advertising is the cash cow. It’s been growing at 10-15% a year. Cloud is growing faster—30-40%—but it’s still a smaller piece of the pie. The question is: can these two engines keep earnings growing at 15% a year for the next five years? I think so. Digital advertising is still taking share from traditional media. TV, radio, print—they’re all shrinking. Google is the biggest beneficiary. And cloud computing is a massive market. Amazon Web Services is the leader, but Google Cloud is growing fast. It’s like the La Quinta story. La Quinta was growing at 20% a year because it had room to expand into new markets. Google Cloud has room to expand into new markets. The difference is that La Quinta was a small company. Google is a giant. But the principle is the same: find the growth driver and bet on it.
