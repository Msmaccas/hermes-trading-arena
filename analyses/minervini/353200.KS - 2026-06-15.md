**SECTION 4: THE VCP FRAMEWORK – APPLYING THE HARD RULES**

Let’s pretend I had a full chart. Here’s how I would apply the VCP rules.

**Rule 1: Each correction wave shows LESS volatility than the last.**  
I would look for a base that has 2-3 pullbacks, each with smaller percentage declines. For example, a 15% drop, then a 10% drop, then a 5% drop. That’s a classic VCP.

**Rule 2: Right side volume drops to 10-20% of average daily.**  
Volume should dry up as the base tightens. If I see volume spiking on the left side (selling) and drying up on the right side (doldrums), that’s accumulation.

**Rule 3: RS line in new high ground before price.**  
This is the strongest tell. If the Relative Strength line breaks out to a new high while the stock is still in the base, that’s a buy signal. It means the stock is outperforming the market.

**Rule 4: Shakeout – undercuts prior low, must rip back within days.**  
I love shakeouts. They flush out weak holders. If a stock undercuts the prior low of the base but then rips back above it within 1-3 days, that’s a sign of institutional buying.

**Rule 5: Prior uptrend – stock has doubled+ from lows.**  
I want to see a strong uptrend before the base. If the stock has gone from $50 to $100, then formed a base, that’s a continuation pattern. If it’s just bouncing from a low, it’s a different story.

**Rule 6: Base length – 5-6 weeks minimum.**  
Shorter bases produce shorter moves. A 3-week base might lead to a 10% gain. A 6-week base can lead to a 50% gain.

**What I would look for in 353200.KS:**  
I would need to see a base that started at least 5-6 weeks ago. I would need to see volume drying up on the right side. I would need to see the RS line breaking out. I would need to see a shakeout that was quickly resolved.
