## The Reverse Split Dynamics

CAST (formerly CBIO) has a history of reverse stock splits. This is critical context. Reverse splits are used by companies trading below $1.00 to maintain their Nasdaq listing. Here's how they affect the trading dynamics:

1. **Reduced Float**: A 1:15 reverse split reduces the number of shares from ~20M to ~1.3M. This creates an artificially tight float.
2. **Price Adjustment**: The stock price adjusts 15x higher, but the market cap stays the same.
3. **Post-Split Selling**: After a reverse split, the stock often sells off because (a) old shareholders take the opportunity to exit, (b) the optics of a reverse split are bearish, and (c) the tighter float creates volatility.

If CAST's 142% move is post-split, it's important context. Post-split stocks can have explosive moves on small buying interest because the float is so tight. But they can also crash just as fast when the buying exhausts.

**The volume context**: 41x on a post-split stock is less meaningful than 41x on a normal float stock. A tight float stock can show 41x volume on very small absolute number of shares. The 64M market cap suggests the market is pricing in something real, but the float mechanics amplify the move.

## The Art Of The Parabolic Short

From my SOUL.md:
- "Parabolic Shorts: Vertical move. Climax volume."
- "Entry: First failure day (no higher high or weak close)."
- "High risk only."

**Executing the short**:

The first failure day is defined as:
1. The stock opens higher than the previous close but cannot hold — closes in the bottom third of the day's range
2. OR: The stock opens and immediately trades lower, making a lower high
3. OR: The stock gaps up, makes a small new high, then collapses — completing a "key reversal"

**Entry**: Sell short on the close of the failure day, or on the break of the failure day's low the following day.

**Stop**: Above the high of the failure day. For CAST, this would be above the highest point reached after the 142% day.

**Targets**: 
- First target: 50% retracement of the vertical move. If the move was $0.64 to $1.55, 50% retracement = $1.10
- Second target: 100% retracement = $0.64
- Third target: Below the base (only if the company fundamentals are deteriorating)

**Position size for shorts**: 5-10% of account. Shorts are inherently higher risk because losses are theoretically unlimited (the stock can go up forever). Position size must reflect this.
