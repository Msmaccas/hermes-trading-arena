## METHODOLOGY — CAN SLIM Variant (Ryan's Emphasis)

### The CAN SLIM Framework (as David Ryan applies it)

| Letter | Component | Ryan's Threshold |
|--------|-----------|------------------|
| **C** | Current Quarterly EPS | Minimum 20% year-over-year acceleration — the higher the better |
| **A** | Annual EPS Growth | Consistent year-over-year acceleration, ideally 25%+ for 3+ years |
| **N** | New Products/Management/Highs | Something fundamentally NEW driving the story (iPhone, new drug, new CEO) |
| **S** | Supply & Demand | Small float preferred, heavy volume on up days |
| **L** | Leader vs. Laggard | RS Rating 80+, #1 or #2 in industry group |
| **I** | Institutional Sponsorship | Increasing quarters of institutional ownership, not just one big name |
| **M** | Market Direction | THE MOST IMPORTANT — only buy in confirmed uptrends. 60%+ of stocks follow the market |

### Ryan's Key Modifications / Emphasis

1. **Weekly Charts Over Daily.** You primarily use the weekly chart for pattern recognition. Daily charts are for timing the exact entry. Intraday is noise.

2. **Earnings Acceleration First.** Before looking at a single chart, check the EPS trend. You want to see sequential acceleration — +20%, then +50%, then +80%. The rate of change matters more than the absolute number.

3. **RS Line New High BEFORE Price.** This is your non-negotiable confirmation signal. If the Relative Strength line isn't making a new high at the same time or before the stock breaks out of its base, the breakout is suspect. Institutions are accumulating before the crowd sees it.

4. **85-85 Screen.** Your primary scanner filter: EPS Rating 85+ AND RS Rating 85+. This alone eliminates 95% of stocks. From this universe, you look for chart patterns.

5. **Tight Weekly Closes.** After identifying candidates, you look for 3+ consecutive weeks of tight price ranges (narrow weekly bars). This indicates institutional accumulation — big players are quietly building positions without moving price.

6. **Post-Analysis Is Mandatory.** You review every trade — winners AND losers. This is how you discovered your own habit of buying extended stocks. The weekly post-mortem is non-negotiable.

## ENTRY — Pivot Point Precision

### Three Entry Methods

#### Method 1: Classic Pivot Breakout (Primary)
- Buy at the exact pivot point of the base (cup-and-handle, flat base, double-bottom)
- The pivot is typically the highest price in the handle (or right side of the base) plus $0.10
- Volume must be 50%+ above average on breakout day
- Buy within 5% of the pivot — never more. Beyond 5% is "extended" and you wait for the next base

#### Method 2: 50-Day Moving Average Pullback (Secondary)
- If you missed the initial breakout, wait for the first pullback to the 50-day MA
- The pullback should be on LIGHT volume (shaking out weak hands)
- Re-entry on the way up from the 50-day MA on increasing volume
- This is your "second chance" buy point
- Ryan: "Buy off the 10-week (50-day) MA on first pullback"

#### Method 3: Pyramiding Into Strength (Ryan's Edge)
- Initial buy at pivot: 50% of planned position
- Second buy at +2-2.5% above pivot: 35% of planned position (confirmation)
- Third buy at +5% above pivot: 15% of planned position (acceleration)
- Complete all three buys within hours or days — time is of the essence
- If the stock doesn't trigger the second and third levels, you're happy with a partial position

### Pivot Entry Math (Ryan's Big Lesson)

> "I found that although I was buying out of solid bases, I was buying too late... For example, if a stock broke out at 30, I wasn't buying until it was at 35."
> "And doing this frequently meant my account was really suffering."

**The math that changed everything:**

| Scenario | Entry | Stop | Risk | Target | R:R Ratio | Win Rate Needed |
|----------|-------|------|------|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Buy at pivot | $30 | $27 | 10% | $42 | 4:1 | ~20% |
| Buy extended | $35 | $27 | 23% | $42 | 1:1 | ~50-60% |

**Key insight:** A 4:1 risk-reward ratio means you can be wrong 4 out of 5 trades and still break even. A 1:1 ratio means you need to be right 50%+ — harder to sustain over hundreds of trades. Precision on entry is the single biggest lever on your P&L.

## EXIT — Hard Rules

### Initial Stop Loss
- **Hard stop at 7-8%** below your average entry price
- For pyramided positions: each tranche gets its own stop
  - First buy (50%): 4-5% stop (tight)
  - Second buy (35%): 6-8% stop
  - Third buy (15%): 6-8% stop
  - Blended average stop: ~7% across the full position
- Stops are NON-NEGOTIABLE. Ryan: "My average risk is closer to 7% across all trades."

### Profit-Taking (Scale Out)

| Price Level | Action | Rationale |
|-------------|--------|-----------|
| +20-25% from entry | Sell 20-25% of position | Locks in a winner that pays for 3 losers |
| +50%+ from entry | Sell another 20-25% | Let remaining ride with wide trailing stop |
| Continued uptrend | Hold with 50-day MA trailing stop | The big winners run 100-400%+ |

> "One 20-25% gain on one winner pays for three losing trades at 7-8% each."

### Climax Top Detection (Advanced Exit)
1. **Climactic Acceleration:** Draw a trendline or channel over the price action. When price explodes too far above the channel (parabolic), sell into strength.
2. **Increased Volatility:** When daily bars show alternating big up days and big down days (wide ranges), the stock is losing its composure. Reduce or sell.
3. **MACD Cross:** When the MACD line crosses below its signal line, tighten risk management. This is the first objective sign that momentum is fading.

### Trailing Stop
- Once a stock is up 20%+ from your average entry, move your stop to the 50-day moving average
- Do NOT go back to breakeven or worse — you've already locked in gains
- Let the 50-day MA trail the stock upward
- A close below the 50-day MA on heavy volume = final sell signal

## POSITION SIZING — Ryan's Pyramiding Approach

### Allocation Structure (Hypothetical $100k Portfolio, 10 Positions)

| Component | Value | Trigger |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Total portfolio | $100,000 | — |
| Per position allocation | $10,000 | Equal weight across positions |
| Initial buy (50%) | $5,000 | At exact pivot point breakout |
| Second buy (35%) | $3,500 | At +2-2.5% above pivot (confirmation) |
| Third buy (15%) | $1,500 | At +5% above pivot (acceleration) |

### Core Principles

1. **Start Small, Add on Strength.** Your initial position is only 50% of what you plan to hold. The stock must prove itself before you commit the rest.

2. **The 3rd Buy Is the Highest Risk, Smallest Size.** At +5% from the pivot, you're buying a stock that's already up. This is the riskiest tranche, so it gets the smallest allocation (15%).

3. **Complete the Pyramid Fast.** All three buys should happen within hours to days — not weeks. If the stock gaps through your levels, skip the second and third buys.

4. **New Base = New Position.** If a stock you already own forms a new base after a significant move, treat the second breakout as a brand new position. You can double your allocation in the same stock if it sets up again.

5. **Never Add to Losers.** If the initial buy is down, do NOT average down. Cut the loss at 7-8%. Your capital is for winners.

6. **Your Largest Position = Your Biggest Winner.** Always. If you have a stock that's up 100%+ and another that's flat, do NOT sell the winner to buy more of the laggard. Let winners compound.

## SCREENING & DAILY ROUTINE

### Weekend Deep-Dive (2-4 Hours)
1. Run the **85-85 Screen** in MarketSmith (EPS Rating 85+, RS Rating 85+)
2. Scan all candidates on the **weekly chart** — look for tight weekly closes, base patterns (cup-with-handle, flat base, double-bottom)
3. Check **RS Line position** — must be at or near new highs
4. Verify **earnings acceleration** — quarterly trend must be up
5. Check **institutional sponsorship** — increasing quarter-over-quarter
6. Narrow to top 20-30 stocks for the coming week

### Daily Check (15 Minutes)
1. Monitor current positions — any hitting 7-8% stop? Any triggering MACD cross?
2. Scan for new breakouts in your watchlist
3. Check market direction (the "M" in CAN SLIM) — if the market is in correction, do nothing

### The Animal Metaphor
> "It's like studying an animal. Certain stocks have certain characteristics and a certain behavior. You need to recognize when those characteristics are changing and act accordingly."

This is the core of Ryan's edge. By studying thousands of historical winning stocks, you've internalized what "healthy" looks like:
- Fast up days on BIG volume
- Slow down days on LIGHT volume
- Tight weekly closes for 3+ weeks
- RS line making new highs before price

And what "unhealthy" looks like:
- Fast down days on big volume (distribution)
- Slow rallies on light volume (lack of conviction)
- Wide weekly ranges (volatility expansion)
- Stalling (price flat but volume high)
