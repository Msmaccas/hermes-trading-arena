## VCP (VOLATILITY CONTRACTION PATTERN) — HARD RULES

### The Core Structure:
1. **Prior uptrend:** Stock has doubled (100%+) or more from its lows before the base forms
2. **Base formation:** Each correction wave shows LESS volatility than the last — contracting ranges
3. **Right side volume:** Volume drops to 10-20% of average daily — "selling vacuum"
4. **RS line:** Must be in NEW HIGH GROUND before price breaks out — strongest tell of institutional accumulation
5. **Shakeout:** May undercut prior low, but MUST rip back within days — tests remaining holders
6. **Base length:** 5-6 weeks minimum. Shorter bases = shorter moves. 8+ weeks preferred for big runners.
7. **Tight closes:** Multiple weeks of tight weekly closes near the highs — "that's picture perfect price and volume action, that's precisely what you want to see"

### The VCP "T" Count — Each Wave Must Tighten in BOTH Price AND Volume:
- Each contraction wave is labeled T1, T2, T3...
- T1 = first correction leg. Typically widest range, heaviest volume.
- T2 = second leg. Must show narrower price range AND lower volume than T1.
- T3 = third leg. Even tighter. Volume nearly invisible.
- T3 or T4 is typically the ideal entry point — the "selling vacuum" is confirmed.
- *"It tightens up real nice here and holds its gains sets up a perfect... it's a perfect cup with handle right vcp what I call it"*

### Selling Vacuum — The Most Important Read:
- *"I want to see that selling vacuum i want to see very few volume uh markers on the downside"*
- *"There is virtually no selling whatsoever once you form this base, it's all blue bars... no selling whatsoever above average volume"*
- Volume on the right side should be nearly invisible — no supply coming to market
- *"There's zero selling on the weekly bars that's exactly what i want to see"*
- Weekly chart is primary: *"I always look at the weekly as well and that's where i really like to look the volume markers because this is where the institutional volume is really going to stick out"*

### Cheat Areas & Expert Buys:
- **Cheat:** A handle/pivot within the middle portion of the base — earlier entry before the full handle forms
- **Low Cheat:** Lower-third pivot within the base — requires expert skill to assess overhead supply
- **Cup Completion Cheat:** When the cup completes before the handle forms
- *"The further you go into the base, the more you're going into overhead supply on the left side... as I developed as a trader I started getting better at recognizing overhead"*
- **Expert Buy:** Only for advanced traders who can read overhead supply to the left

## ENTRY RULES — STRICT

### At the Pivot (Base Breakout):
- Buy at pivot = breach of base high
- Volume MUST expand significantly on breakout day — confirms institutional buying
- Do NOT buy more than 5% above the pivot
- *"The best buy is at a higher price than the previous buy"* — pyramiding up is correct, not averaging down

### Staggered / Pyramiding Entry (1/3 System):
- **1st third** at initial pivot breakout
- **2nd third** at +2-2.5% above pivot (confirmation that breakout is real)
- **3rd third** at +5% above pivot (acceleration phase)
- *"You'd be up 20% on the whole thing"* — ANF trade: +30% on first buy, +17% on second, +11% on third = ~20% blended

### Pilot Position Concept:
- First buy is a "pilot" — small test position
- If pilot works (moves in your favor within 1-3 days) → add
- If pilot fails (hits stop or stalls) → stay small or exit
- *"If our trades aren't working we're not increasing size"*
- *"Doesn't matter the whole world could be doing well if our trades aren't working we're not increasing size"*

### Pullback Entry (Alternate — Only on the Turn):
- *"I'm not actually buying as it's pulling back i'm buying as it's turning up from the pullback"*
- *"I very seldom if ever am buying on the way down i'm always trying to get all the trends all the movement in the direction of my trade"*
- Entry trigger: stock turns up and takes out the prior day's high
- *"Buy when uncertainty is wrung out"* — wait until the base's right side shows zero selling, then buy the first sign of turning

### Weekly Chart Confirmation:
- Only one down bar on the weekly; close above mid-range = accumulation, not selling
- *"It actually had a close that was above the mid of its range so that's not even a down week that's actually an accumulation"*

## EXIT RULES — STRICT

### Cutting Losses — THE KEY (The 72% Revelation):
Mark's career-defining aha moment — he ran the numbers on his actual trading, normalized every loss to a 10% stop. The result: his account would have been UP 72% instead of down. He recalculated twice because he didn't believe it.

*"I had a 15% loss I normalized everything to a 10% stop and my account would have been up 72% and I said wait a minute that has to be wrong... I calculated it again came back the same I calculated a third time lo and behold it was correct"*

That day he drew the Line in the Sand:

*"I said to myself never ever again am I allowing a loss to balloon, I'm cutting a Line in the Sand and that's it"*

*"I'm never getting taken out by a big loss ever again because I was every now and then I'd have a stock blow up or I'd fall in love with a stock and I'd buy more of it when it's down"*

**Hard stop at 7-8% MAXIMUM.** The math:
- Down 20% → takes 25% to get even
- Down 50% → takes 100% to get even
- 8% → ~8.7% to get even — manageable
- *"Losses work geometrically against you"*

**Average loss goal:** 3-5% ideally. *"If we're really tight you know 3% three and a half 4%."* In choppy markets: 5-6%. Hard ceiling: 8% — "8% is a number that goes back to O'Neal."

**Staggered stops:** Sometimes a 4% and a 12% stagger. Max 10-12% on the outer stop. Average stops still come in around 8%.

### Taking Profits — First Sell at 20-25%:
- Target minimum 2:1 reward-to-risk: *"We're shooting for a minimum of two to one"*
- First meaningful sell: 20-25% gain — "sell into strength and nail down a profit"
- Sell into STRENGTH: *"If this stock should start running up here and accelerate its advance i would sell right into the strength and nail down a profit"*
- Natural reactions framework: *"I usually go through anywhere between one and three natural reactions and then i'm looking to sell it into strength"*

### Trail with 50-day Moving Average (Big Winners):
- After the first sell (taking initial profits), let the remainder ride
- Trail stop under the 50-day MA — *"Some of these stocks go up thousands of percent they don't even close below their 50-day moving average let alone the 200 day"*
- Once stock closes decisively below 50-day MA → sell the rest
- This converts trades into asymmetric leverage positions with ZERO risk after the first partial sale

### Failure Reset:
- If a VCP setup breaks down (stock hits stop) BUT the fundamentals/story remain intact → KEEP WATCHING
- Second setup is often MORE reliable than the first
- *"If this stock should start running up here and accelerate its advance I would sell right into the strength"*
- *"If you lose on a trade it's because you missed something either the market the stock it wasn't set up properly something you missed because when you do it right it works perfect"*

## POSITION SIZING — PROGRESSIVE EXPOSURE SYSTEM

### The Exposure Ramp:
| Stage | Account Exposure | Conditions |
|-------|-----------------|------------|
| **Base** | 25% of account | 4-5 positions at ~5% each, or 1-2 at 10-12% (high conviction) |
| **Ramp 1** | 50% | Pilot buys working — add 2-3 more positions |
| **Ramp 2** | 75-100% | Fully deployed — setups abundant, trades performing |
| **Retreat** | Back to 25% or cash | Pilot buys failing — your read on the market is wrong |

### Two Gauges Before Ram:
1. **Are there setups?** *"The best sign is there're gonna be more setups than you have money to buy"*
2. **Are they working?** *"If our trades aren't working we're not increasing size"*

### Maximum Account Risk Per Trade: 1-2%
- Position size × stop distance = max 1-2% of total account at risk
- *"Keep track of your losses in dollars do it in percentage so if you lost 3% on a thousand dollar position or a million dollar position it's still 3% and it removes some of that emotional impact"*

### Correlation Limits:
- Do NOT overload in the same industry group
- Monitor "the axe in the group" — if Nvidia has a major break (biggest down day in 5 years on largest volume), that signals group trouble
- A "cheat" or "handle" within the same base is not a new position — it's pyramiding the same name
- *"Unless it's a key stock and it has a major break... I tend to stay stock by stock"*

### Asymmetric Leverage (The Holy Grail structure):
- *"We're always trying to get to a point... asymmetric leverage where the upside far outweighs the downside to a point where we've improved our worst case scenario where we don't have any risk anymore"*
- How it works: Buy full position → stock up 10% → sell overweight (the extra you added) → now have a full position with zero risk
- *"I have my whole position and I have zero risk — that's what we call asymmetric leverage"*
- This is how you achieve low drawdowns and massive compound returns simultaneously

## RISK — FIVE LEVELS

Risk is more than just your stop:

1. **Stop level** — Where you get out (% or technical)
2. **Liquidity risk** — Can you exit without moving the market?
3. **Gap risk** — Stock gaps through your stop overnight
4. **Position sizing risk** — One stock too large = portfolio at risk of 50% gap down
5. **Spread risk** — Wide bid/ask on illiquid names; entering at a $2 spread on 50,000 shares = $100K starting loss

*"The tighter the risk the better"* — Brandon
