But numbers alone don’t make a winner. You need the pattern, the setup, the market context. Let’s walk through the SEPA framework.

**S — Superperformance Characteristics**

EPS growth of 88% is exactly the kind of acceleration I look for. I want to see that number expanding—not just a one-time pop, but a trend. If this is the second or third quarter of such growth, even better. Revenue should be accelerating too, and in industrials, that often means a catalyst: a new contract, a new technology, a shift in the cycle. I don’t know the exact story here, but the price action and earnings tell me something is working.

As I wrote in *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*, “The biggest mistake investors make is buying cheap stocks that are cheap for a reason.” (Source: https://www.amazon.com/Trade-Like-Stock-Market-Wizard/dp/0071807667) A high P/E with accelerating earnings is a sign of strength, not a red flag.

**E — Exact Entry (The VCP)**

Now, the entry. I need to see a Volatility Contraction Pattern. The price is at $223, with a 50-day MA of $191 and a 200-day MA of $136. That’s a stock in a strong uptrend—the 50-day is well above the 200-day, and both are rising. That’s the Ascending trend template, check.

But I need to see the VCP. The stock has been consolidating recently. I look for at least three contractions, each tighter than the last. The final contraction should have very tight daily ranges on drying volume. The RSI at 62.1 is neutral—not overbought, not oversold. That’s fine. I want to see the volume dry up in the final days of the base. If I see a day with a narrow range and volume 30-40% below the average, that’s my signal.

The volume ratio is 1.08, which is slightly above average. That’s not a problem yet. I want to see volume spike on the breakout day—at least 50% above average, preferably 100% or more. If the stock breaks out on low volume, I’m suspicious.

**P — Proper Position Sizing**

This is where most people blow it. They see a setup and go all in. I start with a pilot position—25% of my intended size. If the stock confirms with a strong follow-through day, I add another 25%. I do this over three or four days. The key is to keep my risk small. My maximum loss per trade is 1-2% of my total account. If I’m wrong, I want to be wrong small.

As I said in *Mindset Secrets for Winning*, “When I’m wrong, I want to be wrong small. When I’m right, I want to be right big.” (Source: https://www.amazon.com/Mindset-Secrets-Winning-Mark-Minervini/dp/1955847005) That’s the mindset. I don’t need to be right all the time. I just need to make money when I am right.

**A — Ascending Trend**

This is the easiest part. The stock is above its 50-day, 150-day, and 200-day moving averages. The 50-day is above the 150-day, and the 150-day is above the 200-day. The 200-day is rising. The stock is within 25% of its 52-week high. The RS line should be rising too—if it’s making a new high before price breaks out, that’s a powerful confirmation.

**Market Breadth**

I don’t trade in a vacuum. I need to know the market environment. If we’re in a confirmed uptrend with strong breadth—70% of stocks above their 50-day MA, 60% above their 200-day, and advancers leading decliners by 2:1—then I’m aggressive. If the market is weak, I’m cautious. Right now, I’d need to check those numbers. But if the market is supportive, STRL could be a prime candidate.
