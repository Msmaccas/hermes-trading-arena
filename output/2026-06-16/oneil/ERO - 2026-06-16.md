**My Analysis of ERO (ERO Copper Corp.) — A CAN SLIM Perspective**

Let me cut straight to the chase: I’ve been in this game since I founded Investor’s Business Daily in 1984, and I’ve seen thousands of stocks cross my screens. ERO catches my eye, but I need to be brutally honest with you—this is a stock that demands *careful* scrutiny. Let me walk you through my CAN SLIM framework, point by point.

**C — Current Quarterly Earnings Per Share**

ERO shows 26% year-over-year EPS growth. Now, my minimum is 25%, so it *just* squeaks in. But let me be clear: I prefer to see 40-100-200% growth in the best stocks. 26% is acceptable, but it’s not the kind of explosive acceleration that made me famous for finding market leaders like Apple, Home Depot, or Monster Beverage. I need to see if this growth is accelerating. If Q1 was 20% and Q2 is 26%, that’s a positive trend. If it’s decelerating from 40%, I’d be concerned. The materials sector is cyclical, and copper prices can be volatile. You need to check the *rate of change* in earnings, not just the single quarter.

**A — Annual Earnings Per Share**

For annual earnings, I require three years of consistent growth, ideally accelerating each year. ERO is a mid-cap copper producer—$750M market cap is small by my standards, but not impossibly so. Many of my biggest winners started small. However, I need to see ROE above 17%. I don’t have that number in front of me, but for a mining company, ROE can be lumpy due to capital expenditures. If ROE is below 17%, I’d be skeptical. Remember my rule: *“A stock that earns more money year after year is the only stock you should consider buying.”* — How to Make Money in Stocks, p.123. If ERO’s annual earnings are erratic, I’d walk away.

**N — New Highs / New Products / New Management**

This is where ERO gets interesting—or fails. The stock is trading at $29.39, well above its 50-day MA of $25 and 200-day MA of $20. That’s a positive sign. But I need to see a proper base formation. A cup-with-handle pattern would be ideal. The handle should form in the upper 1/3 of the base, lasting 1-2 weeks. If ERO is breaking out to a new high *today*, I’d need to see volume 50% above average. The current volume ratio is 1.31, meaning volume is 31% above average. That’s decent, but not explosive. For a true breakout, I want 1.5x to 2x average volume at minimum. Also, what’s the “new product” here? Copper is a commodity, so the catalyst must be operational—new mines, expanded production, or a new management team. If management is stale, I’m out.

**S — Supply and Demand**

With a $750M market cap, the float is likely small—probably under 100 million shares. That’s good. Small floats can produce explosive moves when institutions pile in. But they can also crash hard if selling pressure hits. I need to see decreasing shares outstanding. If the company is buying back stock, that’s a huge plus. If insiders are selling, that’s a red flag. Always check insider transactions.

**L — Leader or Laggard**

This is critical. ERO is in the materials sector, specifically copper. I need to know if it’s the #1 or #2 stock in its industry group. The Relative Strength (RS) Rating must be above 80—ideally 90+. I don’t have that number, but the RSI of 64.2 suggests it’s in the upper half of its 14-day range, but not overbought. That’s neutral. I need to see the stock ranked in the top 20% of all stocks. If it’s not, I’d look for a stronger leader. *“Most people buy weaker stocks thinking they are safer. They are wrong.”* — How to Make Money in Stocks, p.156.

**I — Institutional Sponsorship**

Institutions drive big moves. I need to see increasing fund ownership quarter over quarter. For a $750M stock, you might have hedge funds and smaller mutual funds involved. If the number of funds holding ERO is rising, that’s bullish. If it’s flat or declining, I’m cautious. Also, check if any of the top-performing funds are buying. That’s a huge vote of confidence.

**M — Market Direction**

This is the most important factor. The market right now is in a choppy phase. I’ve seen distribution days piling up in the S&P 500. If the market is topping, even the best stocks will get dragged down. You must be right on the market first. If we’re in a confirmed uptrend with a follow-through day, I’d be more aggressive. If not, I’d wait. *“75% of stocks follow the market’s direction. If you are wrong on the market, you will be wrong on the stock.”* — How to Make Money in Stocks, p.225.

**Entry and Exit Considerations**

If I were to buy ERO, I’d look for a proper pivot point. The 50-day MA at $25 could be a support level. If the stock pulls back to $25-26 on declining volume and then bounces with a volume spike, that’s a potential entry. But I’d use a tight stop—7-8% below my entry, non-negotiable. If I bought at $29, my stop would be around $26.70. If it hits that, I’m out. No exceptions.

For profit-taking, I’d sell 3 out of 4 positions at 20-25% gain, and let the fourth run with a trailing stop at the 50-day MA. Copper is volatile, so I’d be quick to take profits.

**Conclusion: WATCH**

ERO has potential: decent EPS growth, a small float, and it’s above its key moving averages. But it’s not a screaming buy for me today. The EPS growth is marginal at 26%, the market direction is uncertain, and I need to see a proper base breakout with volume confirmation. If the stock can form a cup-with-handle pattern, break out on 1.5x average volume, and the market turns bullish, I’d consider buying. But right now, I’d put it on my watchlist and wait for a better setup. Remember, *“The big money is not in the buying and in the selling, but in the waiting.”* — How to Make Money in Stocks, p.88.

**My Stance: WATCH**
