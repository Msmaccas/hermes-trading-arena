# David Ryan's Analysis: ERO (Ero Copper Corp)

Let me tell you something right off the bat. When I see a stock like ERO at $29.39 with a market cap of only $750 million, my ears perk up. I've made some of my biggest winners in smaller, nimble names that institutions are just starting to discover. But let me walk you through my full checklist before I get too excited.

First, let's talk about what matters most to me — earnings acceleration. And I have to be honest with you, the 26% EPS growth here isn't what I'd call a home run. As I've said many times, "I look for earnings accelerating — 200% growth is better than 100%. And 400% is better than 200%." (IBD Interview, https://www.investors.com/ibd-videos/). Twenty-six percent is decent, but it's not the kind of number that gets me jumping out of my chair. However, I need to dig deeper. Is this number accelerating or decelerating? If last quarter was 15% and now it's 26%, that's acceleration. If it was 40% and now it's 26%, that's a red flag. Without seeing the full quarterly history, I'm cautious but not dismissive.

Now let's look at the technical picture. The stock is trading at $29.39, which is nicely above its 50-day moving average of $25 and well above the 200-day at $20. That's a 17% gain from the 50-day and a 47% gain from the 200-day. This tells me the stock is in a confirmed uptrend. The RSI at 64.2 is perfect — it's not overbought, not oversold. It's in that sweet spot where stocks can continue to run. I've seen too many traders get scared of stocks at RSI 70+ and miss the biggest moves. Let me tell you, some of my biggest winners had RSIs of 80 and kept going.

The volume ratio of 1.31 is interesting. It's above average, which is good, but I'd want to see what that volume is doing. Is it coming on up days or down days? If the stock is moving up on above-average volume, that's accumulation. If it's pulling back on heavy volume, that's distribution. The 1.31 ratio alone doesn't tell me enough. I need to see the daily charts.

Here's what I really like: the market cap at $750 million. As I've said, "I want smaller companies that institutions are just starting to buy. The big money is made in names that go from $500 million to $5 billion, not from $50 billion to $100 billion." This is exactly the kind of name that can double or triple if everything aligns.

The P/E of 19.8 is reasonable for a materials stock. It's not dirt cheap, but it's not expensive either. Remember, I don't buy stocks because they're cheap. I buy them because they're strong and getting stronger. A P/E of 20 on a stock with 26% earnings growth gives you a PEG ratio under 1, which is actually attractive.

Now, let me apply my CAN SLIM framework:

**C - Current Earnings**: 26% growth. This is below my preferred 50% minimum, but if this is accelerating from lower levels, I'd watch it closely. I need to see the quarterly progression.

**A - Annual Earnings**: Without seeing three years of history, I can't fully assess this. But for a copper miner, earnings are cyclical. I'd want to see consistent growth through the cycle.

**N - New Highs/New Products**: Copper is in high demand for electrification and renewable energy. That's a powerful macro tailwind. If ERO is expanding production or has new discoveries, that's the "N" factor working.

**S - Supply**: Only 25 million shares outstanding? That's tiny. That's exactly what I want. Small float stocks can make explosive moves when institutions start buying.

**L - Leader**: I'd need to check the RS rating. At $29.39 with the stock above both moving averages, it's likely 80+. But I want to see if it's the #1 stock in the copper mining group.

**I - Institutional Sponsorship**: This is critical. With only $750M market cap, institutions may just be starting to accumulate. I'd look for funds that have recently added positions, not ones that have been holding for years.

**M - Market Direction**: This is always the first thing I check. If the market is in a confirmed uptrend, I'm aggressive. If we're in distribution, I'm defensive.

Let me give you my honest assessment. This stock has some of the ingredients I look for — small cap, above moving averages, reasonable valuation, and a strong sector tailwind. But the 26% earnings growth is a concern. I've said it before and I'll say it again: "The biggest winners have earnings accelerating quarter after quarter — not just high earnings, but ACCELERATING earnings." (MarketWise Interview, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=83J6JYXZ7Wg).

If I were to take a position, I'd want to see the stock form a proper base — a cup with handle or a flat base — and then break out on volume 50% above average. I'd want to see the next earnings report come in at 40% or higher. And I'd want to confirm that institutions are increasing their positions.

Let me give you my final verdict based on what I see:

**WATCH**

Here's why I'm not giving it a full BUY today. The earnings growth at 26% is below my preferred threshold. I need to see acceleration. The stock is in an uptrend, which is good, but I don't see a clear breakout pattern. The volume is slightly above average but not explosive.

But I'm putting it on my watchlist. If the next quarter shows 40%+ earnings growth, if the stock forms a tight base near these levels, and if volume picks up significantly on a breakout above $30, I'd be a buyer. This is exactly the kind of name that could be a multi-bagger if the earnings start accelerating.

Remember what I always say: "I start with 10,000 stocks. By the time I'm done screening, I have 3-5 candidates. Those are the only ones worth looking at." ERO is in my top 50 right now, but it needs to prove itself before it gets into my top 5.

If you're already in it, hold it as long as it stays above the 50-day moving average. If it breaks below $25 on heavy volume, get out. If it breaks out above $30 on strong volume, add to your position. And if the next earnings report shows acceleration, buy more.

For now, it's a WATCH. Not a BUY, not an AVOID. But I'm watching it closely because this is the kind of name that could turn into a monster if the earnings start to accelerate. And that's what I'm always looking for — the next big winner before everyone else finds it.
