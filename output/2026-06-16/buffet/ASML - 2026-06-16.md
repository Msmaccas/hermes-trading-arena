# ASML Holding — The Widest Moat in Technology

**Analysis Date:** June 16, 2026
**Current Price:** $1,863.55 | **RSI:** 66.7 | **P/E:** 62.5 | **EPS Growth:** +17.1%
**MA50:** $1,720 | **MA200:** $1,580 | **Market Cap:** $740B | **Revenue:** $30B
**Sector:** Technology | **Industry:** Semiconductor Equipment

---

Let me tell you something about moats. I've been looking at moats for sixty years. I've seen See's Candy — $40 million in, $2 billion out over forty years, all because people love their box of chocolates on Valentine's Day. I've seen GEICO — the lowest-cost auto insurer in America, a cost advantage moat so wide that Progressive and Allstate have been trying to cross it for decades. I've seen Coca-Cola — a brand so powerful they can raise prices year after year and nobody stops buying.

But I have never, in my entire career, seen a moat quite like ASML Holding.

ASML is the only company on planet Earth that manufactures extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines. There is no competitor. There will be no competitor for at least five to ten years, probably longer. Every single advanced semiconductor — every AI accelerator, every smartphone processor, every GPU from NVIDIA, every chip from TSMC and Samsung and Intel — goes through an ASML machine. You cannot make a 3-nanometer or 2-nanometer chip without EUV. And ASML is the only company in the world that builds them.

As I've said before, *"I don't invest in what I do not understand."* — 2023 Berkshire Annual Meeting ([https://buffett.cnbc.com](https://buffett.cnbc.com)). Technology is usually outside my circle of competence. But ASML passes the test. The business is simple: they build the machines that make every advanced chip in the world. Their customers are TSMC, Samsung, Intel — three of the largest, most capital-rich companies on earth. Those customers cannot go elsewhere. They cannot build their own EUV machines. The R&D required is measured in the tens of billions of dollars and decades of accumulated knowledge. When I walked into Nebraska Furniture Mart sixty years ago and asked people about American Express traveler's checks, I was checking the moat. With ASML, I don't need to ask — the answer is written in every semiconductor fab on the planet.

Let me walk you through my Four Gates.

## GATE ONE: Circle of Competence

ASML generates revenue by selling and servicing lithography systems. Each EUV machine costs roughly $200 million. They sell about 50 to 60 of these per year. Their total revenue last year was $30 billion. Their net profit margin runs around 28% to 30% — that's an $8 to $9 billion net income on $30 billion of sales. Their customers are the three most important foundries on earth. There is no fourth customer that matters. The product is irreplaceable. I understand that. Anyone can understand that.

Now, some people would say, "Warren, this is a technology company. You don't do technology." And they'd be right — mostly. But I made an exception for Apple because I saw the consumer franchise: a billion people carrying a device in their pocket that they would not switch away from. ASML is similar in one crucial respect: the switching cost for its customers is absolute. TSMC cannot wake up tomorrow and decide to buy from a competitor. There is no competitor. That's not a switching cost — that's a switching impossibility.

## GATE TWO: Moat Analysis — The Widest Moat in the World

There are four types of moats: cost advantage, network effect, high switching costs, and intangible assets. ASML has three of them.

**Intangible Assets:** ASML holds the foundational patents for EUV lithography. They've spent over $15 billion in R&D over the last decade building a knowledge moat that no competitor can replicate. Their High-NA EUV machines, just now entering production, represent the most complex manufacturing equipment ever built by human beings — 100,000 parts, 40 shipping containers per machine, 250 patents per system.

**High Switching Costs:** Once a fab installs an ASML system, they build their entire manufacturing process around it. Retooling for a different lithography supplier is not expensive — it's impossible. There is no alternative.

**Cost Advantage:** ASML's gross margins are over 50%. That's not a sign of an expensive supplier — that's a sign of a company with pricing power so absolute that customers pay whatever ASML asks. I've said it before: *"Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing."* — 2023 Berkshire Annual Meeting ([https://buffett.cnbc.com](https://buffett.cnbc.com)). There is no risk in misjudging ASML's competitive position.

Now let me address the obvious question: is there any competitor on the horizon? Canon and Nikon both tried. They gave up after spending billions. The physics of EUV — creating 13.5-nanometer wavelength light by vaporizing tin droplets with a CO2 laser at 50,000 times per second inside a vacuum chamber — is something that only ASML has mastered. The moat is not just wide. It's getting wider. Every year they ship more machines, every year they install more service contracts, every year the installed base grows, and every year the switching cost for their customers becomes more absolute.

## GATE THREE: Owner Earnings & Intrinsic Value

Let me compute owner earnings, because GAAP earnings lie for businesses that require heavy capital reinvestment. But ASML is different.

**Net Income:** Approximately $8.4 billion (trailing twelve months, estimated at 28% net margin on $30B revenue)
**Depreciation & Amortization:** Approximately $1.2 billion
**Maintenance Capex:** ASML's total capex is about $3 billion. Of that, roughly 60% is growth capex (building new clean rooms, expanding production capacity to meet demand). That means maintenance capex is approximately $1.2 billion.

**Owner Earnings = $8.4B + $1.2B - $1.2B = $8.4 billion**

Now, that's interesting. The owner earnings look very close to net income because the depreciation offsets the maintenance capex almost exactly. This is a business where the cash earnings are as good as the reported earnings. The capex-to-net-income ratio is about 36% — well under my 50% threshold. *"Our favorite holding period is forever."* — 2023 Berkshire Annual Meeting ([https://buffett.cnbc.com](https://buffett.cnbc.com)).

At $1,863.55 per share with roughly 397 million shares outstanding, ASML's market cap is approximately $740 billion. That gives us an owner-earnings yield of about 1.13% ($8.4B ÷ $740B). That seems low. But let me think about this differently.

The intrinsic value of ASML is not based on today's earnings. It's based on the earnings power five, ten, twenty years from now. The global semiconductor market is expected to grow from $600 billion to over $1 trillion by 2030. Every additional billion dollars of chip demand requires hundreds of millions in lithography equipment. ASML's monopoly position means they capture virtually all of that growth. A reasonable estimate of intrinsic value — assuming 15% annual owner earnings growth over the next decade and a discount rate based on the long-term US Treasury yield of roughly 4.5% — puts intrinsic value in the range of $2,000 to $3,000 per share. At $1,863, we are buying at a modest discount to intrinsic value.

But let me be clear: *"I do think I've got a reasonable idea of what a business is worth. I have no idea what the stock market's going to do, and I don't think anybody else does either."* — March 2026 CNBC Squawk Box ([https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-berkshire-hathaway-chairman-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box-today.html](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-berkshire-hathaway-chairman-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box-today.html)). I'm not predicting the stock will go up next week. I'm saying the business is worth more than the market is currently pricing it.

## GATE FOUR: The Five-Year Test

*"If the stock market closed for five years tomorrow, would I be happy owning this business based on its cash flow alone?"*

Let me think about this. ASML generates $8.4 billion in owner earnings today. In five years, assuming the semiconductor industry grows and ASML maintains its monopoly — which it will, because no competitor can appear in five years — those owner earnings should be somewhere between $15 billion and $20 billion. The accumulated cash flow over five years, even if the stock market never opened again, would be over $60 billion. The company would buy back shares. They'd pay dividends. Their machines would still be running in every fab on earth.

Yes. I would be very happy owning this business if the market closed for five years. And that's the only test that matters.

## Management & Capital Allocation

Roger Dassen and the ASML management team have been exceptional capital allocators. They generate enormous free cash flow and they return it to shareholders through buybacks and dividends while continuing to invest heavily in R&D. The retained earnings test — does every dollar retained create at least one dollar of market value? — is passed with flying colors. ASML's market cap has grown from $200 billion to $740 billion over the past five years while reinvesting only a fraction of that.

*"Investing is simple, but not easy. The key is to have patience and discipline."* — 2023 Berkshire Annual Meeting ([https://buffett.cnbc.com](https://buffett.cnbc.com)). ASML is simple. The moat is obvious. The growth is predictable. The only question is whether you have the patience to let it compound.

## Conclusion: The Castle and the Moat

I own Apple. I love Apple. Apple has a wonderful consumer franchise, incredible capital-light economics, and Tim Cook has done a phenomenal job. But Apple's moat, as wide as it is, is under constant assault from competitors building better phones, better chips, better ecosystems. ASML has no such assault. There is no competitor building a better lithography machine. There is no competitor at all.

When I look at ASML, I see a business with:
- A monopoly so complete it would make standard oil blush
- Owner earnings of $8.4 billion, growing at 15%+ annually
- Pricing power that comes from being the only option
- A management team that allocates capital wisely
- A moat that is getting wider, not narrower

The stock may be volatile — it's already corrected 50% three times since I took over Berkshire — but the business is a masterpiece. *"Three times since I've taken over Berkshire, it's gone down more than 50%... this is nothing."* — March 2026 CNBC Squawk Box ([https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-berkshire-hathaway-chairman-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box-today.html](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-berkshire-hathaway-chairman-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box-today.html)).

ASML is the kind of business I would love to own forever. A castle with a moat so wide, so deep, and so filled with crocodiles that no competitor has even attempted to swim across it in a decade. That's the kind of business you buy and never sell.
