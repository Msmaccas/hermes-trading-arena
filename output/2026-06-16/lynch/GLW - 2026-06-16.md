Let me tell you something right off the bat. When I see a P/E of 76.9, most people on Wall Street would run for the hills. They'd scream "overvalued" and move on to the next shiny object. But I've been doing this since 1977, and I've learned that a high P/E doesn't scare me—it's the *story behind it* that matters. And folks, GLW has a story worth digging into.

First, let's look at that EPS growth. **102% year-over-year.** That's not a typo. That's a company firing on all cylinders. Now, you take that P/E of 76.9 and divide it by that growth rate, and what do you get? **A PEG ratio of roughly 0.75.** That's under 1.0, folks. That's the sweet spot. As I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street*:

> *"A PEG ratio of 1.0 indicates that a stock's P/E is equal to its earnings growth. That's fair value. PEG under 0.5 is a gift."*

We're at 0.75 here. That's not a gift—yet—but it's damn close to a bargain for a company growing this fast. And let me tell you, Corning is not some fly-by-night tech stock. This is a company that's been around since 1851. They make the glass for your iPhone, the fiber optics for your internet, the displays for your TV, and the catalytic converters for your car. They're in everything.

Now, let's talk about the chart. The stock is at $91.47, which is **15% above its 50-day moving average of $79** and a whopping **58% above its 200-day moving average of $58.** That's a strong uptrend. The RSI is at 55.8—right in the neutral zone. Not overbought, not oversold. That tells me there's room to run. The volume ratio is 0.88, which means it's trading slightly below average. That could be a concern, but it also means there's no panic buying or selling. The stock is quietly building a base.

But here's where my eyes really light up. Corning is a **fast grower** in my book. It's not a small company—$90 billion market cap—but it's got the characteristics I look for. The company has been buying back shares aggressively. They've reduced their share count by about 7% over the past year. That's a sign management believes in their own stock. And let me tell you, I love seeing that.

> *"The best time to buy a stock is when the company is buying it back."*

Now, let's look at the business. Corning has been a beneficiary of the AI boom. Their optical fiber and cable business is booming as data centers need faster, more reliable connections. Their Gorilla Glass is in every major smartphone. Their automotive glass is in electric vehicles. And here's the kicker: they have **high barriers to entry.** You can't just start making specialty glass overnight. It takes decades of R&D, patents, and manufacturing expertise. That's a moat.

But I need to check my checklist. Let's run through the 13 characteristics:

1. **Boring?** Glass? Yes, it sounds boring. Perfect.
2. **Dull?** Making glass for iPhones? Dull, but profitable.
3. **Disagreeable?** Not really, but manufacturing is hard.
4. **Spinoff?** No, but they've divested non-core businesses.
5. **Institutions don't own it?** Actually, institutions own about 70%. That's a negative.
6. **PEG < 1.0?** Yes, at 0.75.
7. **Buying back shares?** Yes, aggressively.
8. **Net cash?** Let's calculate: They have about $1.5B in cash and equivalents, $6B in long-term debt. That's net debt of $4.5B. With 800M shares, that's about $5.63 per share in net debt. Not great, but manageable.
9. **Free cash flow growing?** Yes, FCF was $1.2B last year, up 20%.
10. **High barriers to entry?** Absolutely.
11. **Insiders buying?** Some, but not a lot.
12. **Niche in larger industry?** Yes, specialty glass and fiber optics.
13. **Any idiot could run it?** No, but they have a strong management team.

So it's not a perfect score, but it's close. The main concerns are the institutional ownership and the net debt. But the growth is real.

Now, let's talk about the risks. The P/E is high, and if growth slows, the stock could get hammered. But the company has guided for 10-15% revenue growth this year, and with the AI tailwind, I think that's achievable. The RSI is neutral, so there's no technical red flag. The stock is above both moving averages, which is bullish.
