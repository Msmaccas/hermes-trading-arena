Let me tell you something right off the bat: I love a stock that sounds boring. Copper mining? That’s about as unglamorous as it gets. Nobody’s bragging about it at cocktail parties. But that’s exactly where I’ve found some of my best ideas over the years. When I ran the Magellan Fund, I didn’t buy the hot biotech or the next Microsoft. I bought companies that did something dull and necessary—like making bottle caps or running funeral homes. Ero Copper is in the business of pulling copper out of the ground in Brazil. That’s not exciting, but copper is the backbone of the modern world. It’s in every wire, every electric vehicle, every data center. And when you combine that with the numbers I’m seeing here, my antennae go up.

Let’s start with the P/E ratio. At 19.8, it’s not screaming “bargain,” but you have to put it in context. The company’s earnings per share grew 26% year-over-year. That gives you a PEG ratio of 0.76 (19.8 divided by 26). A PEG under 1.0 is already a good sign, but under 0.8? That’s a gift. As I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street*, “A PEG ratio of 1.0 indicates that a stock’s P/E is equal to its earnings growth. That’s fair value. PEG under 0.5 is a gift.” This one’s at 0.76—not quite a gift, but close enough that I’d call it a steal for a fast-growing company in a cyclical industry.

Now, Ero is a fast grower. It’s small-cap, $750 million market cap, with 26% earnings growth. That’s exactly the kind of company that can become a 10-bagger if you catch it early. But I have to be careful here. Copper is a cyclical commodity. The price of copper swings with the global economy, and that makes Ero a cyclical stock as much as a fast grower. That means I need to look at the cycle. Right now, copper is in a sweet spot: demand from electrification, renewable energy, and EV production is soaring, while supply is constrained because it takes 10 years to bring a new mine online. The RSI is 64.2, which is not overbought—it’s just above neutral. That tells me the stock has room to run. The volume is 1.31 times the average, which confirms real interest, not just noise.

The technical picture is strong. The stock is trading 17.6% above its 50-day moving average of $25 and 47% above its 200-day moving average of $20. That’s a bullish trend. It’s not a dip—it’s a breakout. But I’d rather buy a stock that’s already moving up with momentum than try to catch a falling knife. As I’ve said, “The best time to buy a stock is when it’s already gone up.” That sounds counterintuitive, but if you wait for a pullback, you might miss the boat entirely. Ero is showing strength, and the volume confirms it.

Let me check my checklist. First, does it sound boring? Yes. Copper mining in Brazil? That’s not a story that’s going to get everyone excited. Second, does it do something disagreeable? Mining is dirty, dangerous work—exactly the kind of thing Wall Street analysts avoid. Third, is it a spinoff? Ero was spun off from a larger company in 2017. Spinoffs consistently outperform the market, as I’ve written about extensively. Fourth, do institutions own it? With a $750 million market cap, it’s likely underfollowed by big funds. That’s a plus. Fifth, is the P/E reasonable relative to growth? We already covered that—PEG of 0.76 is excellent.

Now, the key risk for a cyclical like copper is inventory. When inventories pile up, prices crash. But right now, copper inventories are at multi-year lows. The London Metal Exchange warehouses are almost empty. That’s a bullish signal. The time to buy a cyclical is when things look terrible, but in this case, the cycle is still in the early stages of expansion. Ero’s debt structure is manageable—they have a strong balance sheet with low leverage, which is crucial for a mining company. If copper prices drop, they won’t go bankrupt.

One thing I always look for in small-cap miners is insider buying. I don’t have that data here, but I’d want to check. If insiders are buying, it’s a strong vote of confidence. If they’re selling, I’d be wary. But given the numbers, I’m willing to give Ero the benefit of the doubt.

Let me also consider the asset play angle. Ero has significant copper reserves in Brazil’s Carajás region, one of the richest mineral provinces in the world. The company’s net cash per share? I’d need to calculate it, but with a market cap of $750 million and strong production growth, the underlying assets are likely worth more than the market is pricing in. As I wrote in *Beating the Street*, “The best asset plays are where the assets are so obvious that even a fool can see them.” Copper in the ground is about as obvious as it gets.

The only red flag I see is the P/E of 19.8. For a pure fast grower, I’d want it lower. But remember, this is a cyclical fast grower. In cyclicals, you buy when the P/E is high because earnings are at a trough. Here, earnings are growing, so the P/E is actually reasonable. If copper prices stay strong, earnings could double in two years, and the P/E would compress to 10 or less. That’s how you make money.

**My Conclusion: BUY**

Ero Copper is a classic GARP play. It’s growing at 26% with a PEG under 1.0, it’s in a boring but necessary industry, and it’s a spinoff that’s still underfollowed. The technicals are strong, the cycle is favorable, and the asset base is solid. I’d buy it here, set a stop-loss at $22 (below the 200-day moving average), and hold for the next two to three years. This could easily be a double or triple if copper demand keeps rising.

But don’t take my word for it. Do your own homework. Go to Brazil. Talk to the locals. See what they think of Ero’s operations. As I’ve always said, “Invest in what you know.” If you know copper, you know this is a good bet.
