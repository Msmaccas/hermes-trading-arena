**Analysis of ASM.AS (ASM International) — Through My Eyes**

Let me tell you something right off the bat: when I see a stock with a P/E of 50.9, my first instinct is to throw it in the trash. That’s what the crowd does. But I’ve made my career by looking past the headlines and digging into the numbers that matter. ASM International is a semiconductor equipment company based in the Netherlands, and it’s trading at $632.40 with a market cap of $20 billion. The P/E is sky-high, but the EPS growth is 25% year-over-year. That gives us a PEG ratio of 50.9 / 25 = 2.04. That’s above my threshold of 1.0, and frankly, it’s above 2.0, which I usually tell people to avoid. But hold on—let me walk you through why this might be a *WATCH* rather than an *AVOID*, and why I’m not throwing it out just yet.

First, the category. ASM is a fast grower—25% EPS growth puts it squarely in that 20-25%+ bucket where I look for 10-baggers. But here’s the catch: fast growers are dangerous when the growth story gets overhyped. The RSI is 68.5, which is near overbought territory, and the stock is trading well above its 50-day moving average of $498 and its 200-day moving average of $349. That’s a massive run-up—almost 80% above the 200-day MA. That’s the kind of move that makes me nervous because it means the market has already priced in a lot of optimism. Volume is slightly below average (ratio 0.95), which suggests the buying pressure isn’t accelerating. That could be a yellow flag.

Now, let’s apply my checklist. The first thing I look for in a fast grower is the PEG ratio. At 2.04, this stock is expensive relative to its growth. But here’s where I’d push back: the semiconductor equipment industry is cyclical, and ASM is a niche player in atomic layer deposition (ALD) and epitaxy—two technologies that are critical for advanced chip manufacturing. The company has a strong moat because its tools are used in the most complex production processes. That’s a positive. Also, the company has been buying back shares, which is a good sign—it shows management believes in the intrinsic value. I don’t have the exact buyback data in front of me, but I’d want to check the net cash position: (cash + equivalents - long-term debt) / shares outstanding. If that’s positive and growing, it’s a green light.

But let’s talk about the industry. As I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street*: *"The best stock to buy is the one that is the most undervalued. The worst stock to buy is the one that is the most overvalued."* (Source: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403, p. 187). Right now, ASM is overvalued by my PEG metric. But I also said: *"Go for a business that any idiot can run—because sooner or later, any idiot probably will."* (Source: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403, p. 194). ASM isn’t an idiot-proof business—it’s highly technical, and its success depends on continued innovation and customer relationships. That’s a risk.

The bullish case: ASM is a leader in ALD, which is a high-barrier-to-entry niche. The company has decades of experience, and its customers are the world’s largest chipmakers (TSMC, Intel, Samsung). The global semiconductor market is expected to grow 10-15% annually for the next few years, driven by AI, 5G, and automotive chips. ASM’s revenue growth is likely to outpace that because its tools are used in the most advanced nodes (3nm, 2nm). If EPS growth accelerates to 30% or more, the PEG would drop to 1.7, which is still high but more palatable. The MA50 and MA200 are both rising, which is a bullish technical pattern.

The bearish case: The P/E of 50.9 is unsustainable unless growth accelerates dramatically. If growth slows to 15%, the PEG jumps to 3.4, and you’re in dangerous territory. The RSI is high, and the stock has already had a huge run. Volume is below average, which could mean the rally is losing steam. Also, the semiconductor cycle is historically boom-bust. If we enter a downturn, ASM could drop 40-50% easily. I’ve seen it happen with cyclicals.

Here’s my verdict: This is not a buy at $632.40. The PEG is too high, and the technicals suggest the stock is extended. But I’m not saying avoid it forever. ASM is a high-quality company in a growing industry. If the stock pulls back to the $500 range (near the 50-day MA), the PEG would improve to around 1.6 (assuming growth holds). That’s still above my ideal of 1.0, but for a niche leader with a moat, it’s worth watching. I’d also want to see insider buying and a strong net cash position before pulling the trigger.

**My Stance: WATCH**

I’m not buying here. I’d put this on my radar and wait for a pullback. If it drops 20% to around $500, I’d revisit the numbers. If growth accelerates to 30%+ and the PEG drops below 1.5, I’d consider a small position. But for now, the price is too high, and the story is too well-known. Remember what I said: *"The best time to buy a cyclical is when things are terrible."* (Source: *One Up On Wall Street*, p. 202). Things aren’t terrible for ASM—they’re great. That’s exactly when you have to be careful.
