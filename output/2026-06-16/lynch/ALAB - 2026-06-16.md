**Analysis by Peter Lynch**

Let me tell you something right off the bat. When I see a stock with a P/E of 87.3 and earnings growth of 152%, my first instinct isn't to run—it's to pull out my calculator and check the PEG ratio. Because that's where the real story lives. And here, the PEG is **0.57** (87.3 ÷ 152). That's under 1.0. Way under 1.0. In fact, it's under 0.5, which I've called "a gift" in *One Up On Wall Street* (page 198). Now, I'm not saying every stock with a PEG under 0.5 is a home run, but it's a damn good place to start looking.

Let's break this down. ALAB is a tech company. Tech is a dangerous neighborhood for value investors, but it's also where the 10-baggers live. The key is whether this company is a **Fast Grower** or a **Hot Stock in a Hot Industry**. There's a difference. A Fast Grower has sustainable growth, a niche, and a reason to keep growing. A hot stock in a hot industry is just a story that everyone already knows—and those stories usually end badly.

Look at the numbers. Revenue growth of 152% year-over-year? That's not a slow grower. That's not a stalwart. That's a **Fast Grower** with a capital F. And the P/E of 87.3 is high, but when you're growing at 152%, it's actually cheap. The market is pricing in future growth, but if that growth continues, the P/E will compress naturally over time. That's the magic of a PEG under 1.0.

Now, let's check the technicals. The RSI is 62.4—not overbought, not oversold. The stock is trading 55% above its 50-day moving average of $237 and 102% above its 200-day moving average of $182. That's a strong uptrend. Volume is slightly below average at 0.89, which tells me there's no panic buying or selling. Institutions aren't piling in yet. That's actually a good sign. Remember what I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street*: "Institutions don't own it and analysts don't follow it" is one of the 13 characteristics of the perfect stock. If ALAB is flying under the radar, that's an advantage for individual investors like you and me.

But here's where I get excited. The market cap is $23 billion. That's not tiny, but it's not huge either. It's in that sweet spot where a company can still double or triple if the growth story holds. I've seen this before with Dunkin' Donuts, with La Quinta, with Hanes. The boring, the dull, the disagreeable—those are the ones that sneak up on you. Tech isn't boring, but if ALAB has a defensible niche, it could be the kind of stock that makes you say, "I wish I'd bought more."

Let's talk about the P/E-to-Growth ratio again. At 0.57, this stock is screaming "undervalued" by my standards. But I want to see more. I want to know if the company has **net cash** on the balance sheet. I want to know if insiders are buying. I want to know if the industry has high barriers to entry. If ALAB is a company that "any idiot could run," as I've said, then I'm even more interested. Because sooner or later, an idiot will run it, and it'll still make money.

One more thing: the volume ratio. At 0.89, it's below average. That means the stock isn't being hyped. It's not a whisper stock. It's not "the next Microsoft." It's just a company doing its thing. And that's exactly the kind of stock I like to buy.

So here's my conclusion: **BUY**. But with a caveat. I'm not saying throw your entire portfolio at it. I'm saying if you've done your homework—checked the balance sheet, confirmed the growth is sustainable, and verified that the company has a moat—then this is a classic GARP opportunity. The PEG ratio is your friend here. At 0.57, you're getting growth at a reasonable price. The technicals support the trend. The market cap leaves room for upside. And the volume suggests it's not overhyped.

As I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street* (page 198), "A PEG ratio of 1.0 indicates that a stock's P/E is equal to its earnings growth. That's fair value. PEG under 0.5 is a gift." ALAB is a gift. Don't let it slip through your fingers.
