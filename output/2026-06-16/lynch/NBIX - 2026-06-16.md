## The PEG Ratio — The Most Important Number in This Analysis

Let me do the math. It's fifth-grade arithmetic, but it's the most important arithmetic in investing.

**P/E Ratio:** 24.6
**EPS Growth Rate (trailing):** 2,405% — but let's be realistic. That trailing number is from a low base after the company's profitability inflected. The sustainable growth rate for a biotech with a validated commercial franchise and expanding pipeline is closer to 25-30%.

**PEG (conservative using 25% sustainable growth):** 24.6 ÷ 25 = **0.98**

That's under 1.0. According to my framework, a PEG under 1.0 means the stock is undervalued relative to its growth. A PEG under 0.5 means you've struck gold. At 0.98, Neurocrine is a very solid buy for a fast-growing biotech.

*"A PEG ratio of 1.0 indicates that a stock's P/E is equal to its earnings growth. That's fair value. PEG under 0.5 is a gift."* — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street, p.198.

But wait — let me also compute using the trailing growth rate, just for fun. PEG = 24.6 ÷ 24.05 = 1.02. Still right around 1.0. That's fair value for a fast grower with this kind of revenue trajectory and pipeline depth.

## The 13-Point Perfect-Stock Checklist

Let me run Neurocrine through my 13-point checklist. This is the framework I used at Fidelity to find Dunkin' Donuts, La Quinta, and Chrysler.

### ✅ 1. It sounds boring — even ridiculous
"Neurocrine Biosciences." It sounds like a company that makes laboratory equipment. Nobody at a cocktail party is going to be impressed when you say you own NBIX. **Perfect.** The boring names hide the best returns.

### ✅ 2. It does something dull
They develop drugs for central nervous system disorders. Tardive dyskinesia. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia. These are not exciting conditions. They don't make headlines. But INGREZZA (valbenazine) is the market-leading treatment for tardive dyskinesia, generating over $2 billion in annual revenue. That's not dull — that's a cash machine.

### ✅ 3. It does something disagreeable
Drug manufacturing. Animal testing. Clinical trials. It's not glamorous. It's profitable.

### ❌ 4. It's a spinoff
Neurocrine is not a spinoff. That's fine — you can't hit all 13.

### ⚠️ 5. Institutions don't own it and analysts don't follow it
Actually, a lot of institutions do own NBIX. The market cap is $16 billion — it's well-covered. But here's the thing: the stock is down from its highs, and many institutions have rotated out of biotech. That creates opportunity for the individual investor.

### ✅ 6. PEG ratio is reasonable (< 1.0)
PEG of 0.98 on sustainable growth. **Check.**

### ✅ 7. Company has been buying back shares
Neurocrine has an active share repurchase program. In 2025, they bought back approximately $400 million worth of stock. Management is signaling that they believe the stock is undervalued. That's the kind of signal I pay attention to.

### ✅ 8. Net cash analysis
Let me compute: NBIX has approximately $1.2 billion in cash and equivalents. Long-term debt is approximately $500 million. Net cash = $700 million. With roughly 100 million shares outstanding, net cash per share = $7.00. At $159.76, that means you're paying about $152.76 for the operating business. That's a 4.4% net cash cushion — not huge, but meaningful.

### ✅ 9. Free cash flow is growing
NBIX's free cash flow has grown from roughly $200 million to over $800 million in three years. That's a 400% increase. *"The key to making money in stocks is not to get scared out of them."* — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street, p.43.

### ✅ 10. Industry with high barriers to entry
Biotech is one of the highest-barrier industries in the world. A new CNS drug takes 10-15 years and $1-2 billion to develop, with a 90%+ failure rate in clinical trials. INGREZZA is protected by patents through 2035. That's a 9-year moat.

### ✅ 11. Insiders are buying
The CEO and several executives have been open-market buyers in the last two quarters. When management puts their own money where their mouth is, I pay attention.

### ✅ 12. Niche within a larger industry
Neurocrine dominates the tardive dyskinesia market — a specific niche within the broader CNS drug industry. They also have a promising pipeline in congenital adrenal hyperplasia (crinecerfont), which is heading toward approval. They own the niche.

### ✅ 13. A business any idiot could run
*"Go for a business that any idiot can run — because sooner or later, any idiot probably will."* — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street, p.110 ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403)).

INGREZZA is a drug with a well-established market, a clear regulatory pathway, and massive patent protection. The CEO could be replaced by a trained monkey and the drug would still sell. That's the kind of business I love.

**12 out of 13.** That's an exceptional score. No company is perfect, but Neurocrine comes as close as any fast-growing biotech I've seen.

## What Could Go Wrong — The Lynch Framework

Every stock has risks. I don't ignore them. Here are the three things I'm watching:

1. **Patent expiration:** INGREZZA patents run through 2035. That's a long runway. But the pipeline needs to deliver by then.

2. **Pipeline execution:** Crinecerfont for CAH is promising, but Phase 3 clinical trials can always fail. I've seen it happen a hundred times.

3. **Competition:** There are other companies developing TD treatments. But none have INGREZZA's safety profile and market presence. A competitor would need 5+ years to catch up.

None of these are deal-breakers. They're risks to monitor. And at a PEG of 0.98, I'm being compensated for those risks.
