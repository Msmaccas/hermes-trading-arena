### Let Me Tell You Why This Stock Has My Attention

You know, when I ran the Magellan Fund, I used to say that the best place to find a ten-bagger is in your own backyard. Not in some hot tech IPO that everyone on Wall Street is whispering about, but in a company that’s doing something dull, something that no one wants to talk about at a cocktail party. And that’s exactly what Sterling Infrastructure feels like to me.

At first glance, $8 billion market cap, P/E of 33.4, and an RSI of 62.1—that’s not screaming “bargain bin.” But here’s the thing: I don’t buy stocks based on yesterday’s headlines. I buy them based on what I can see in the numbers and the story behind them. And this story is a classic GARP candidate—Growth at a Reasonable Price.

Let’s start with the growth. EPS growth year-over-year is 88%. That’s not a typo. That’s not a one-time tax benefit. That’s real earnings expansion from a company that’s building the backbone of America’s infrastructure—roads, bridges, data centers, and e-commerce distribution hubs. In my book *One Up On Wall Street*, I wrote:

> *“The best stock to buy is the one you already own.”*  
> — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street (Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403)

And I mean that. If you already own Sterling, you don’t sell it just because the P/E looks a little high. You look at the PEG ratio. That’s the key. P/E of 33.4 divided by 88% growth gives you a PEG of 0.38. A PEG under 0.5 is, in my experience, an extreme undervaluation. It’s a gift. And that’s exactly what we have here. This stock is growing faster than its multiple suggests.

Now, let’s talk about the category. Sterling is a cyclical company, but not in the way you’d think. It’s not an auto or steel cyclical that lives and dies by GDP. It’s a **fast grower** in a cyclical industry. The key to investing in fast growers is to find companies that have a sustainable competitive advantage—a niche. Sterling has that. They’re not just any construction company. They’re heavily exposed to the **e-commerce and data center boom**. Think about it: Amazon, Google, Microsoft—they’re all building massive distribution centers and cloud data hubs. Who’s pouring the concrete and laying the foundations? Sterling.

That’s a niche within a larger industry. And I love that. In *One Up On Wall Street*, I wrote:

> *“Go for a business that any idiot can run — because sooner or later, any idiot probably will.”*  
> — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street (Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403)

Sterling fits that perfectly. Infrastructure is boring. It’s not sexy. You don’t see teenagers wearing Sterling T-shirts. But that’s exactly the kind of company that can compound for years without attracting the attention of every hedge fund on the street.

Now, let’s look at the technicals. The stock is trading at $223, well above its 50-day moving average of $191 and its 200-day moving average of $136. That’s a strong uptrend. The RSI at 62.1 is not overbought—it’s in the sweet spot. Volume is slightly above average at 1.08, which tells me there’s genuine interest, not just noise. The stock has already doubled from its 200-day MA, but that doesn’t scare me. When a company is growing earnings at 88% a year, the stock should be going up. The question is: is the price still reasonable relative to the growth? Yes. Absolutely.

Let’s check my checklist. The 13 characteristics of the perfect stock. Sterling scores high on several:

- **It sounds boring** — Infrastructure. Concrete. Data centers. Yawn. Perfect.
- **It does something dull** — Building the stuff that makes the modern world work.
- **Institutions don’t own it heavily** — At $8B market cap, it’s still small enough that many large funds can’t buy it yet. That’s an advantage.
- **PEG is under 1.0** — We already calculated 0.38. That’s screaming value.
- **Free cash flow** — I’d need to see if it’s growing, but given the earnings growth, it likely is.
- **Insider buying** — I’d check that, but the momentum suggests confidence.

One thing I always look for in fast growers is **sustainable growth**. Can Sterling keep growing at 88%? Probably not forever. But even if growth slows to 30% or 40%, a P/E of 33.4 is still reasonable. And if they maintain a PEG under 1.0, you’re looking at a multi-year compounder.

The biggest risk with fast growers is that they stumble. Two quarters of decelerating same-store sales—or in Sterling’s case, declining backlog—and the stock gets hammered. But right now, with infrastructure spending being a bipartisan priority, and the data center buildout still in its early innings, I think Sterling has room to run.

Let me be clear: I’m not saying this is a no-brainer. No stock is. But if you’re looking for a company that’s growing earnings at a torrid pace, has a reasonable valuation relative to that growth, and operates in a boring but essential industry, Sterling fits the bill.

Remember what I always said: *“Invest in what you know.”* If you know that e-commerce isn’t going away, that data centers need power and cooling and concrete, and that America’s infrastructure is crumbling and needs rebuilding, then you know Sterling.

### My Verdict

**BUY** — But with a caveat. Don’t go all in. Buy a starter position. If the stock pulls back to the 50-day moving average around $191, that’s a gift. If it keeps running, you’ll still have a great entry. This is a stock I’d hold for 3 to 5 years, not 3 to 5 weeks. The PEG ratio tells me the market hasn’t fully priced in the growth. That’s where the alpha is.

**BUY. Not a watch. Not an avoid. A buy.**
