Alright, let me put on my Fidelity Magellan cap and take a hard look at this one. When I see a stock trading at $250 with a P/E of 137, my first instinct is to roll my eyes and move on. But that's what the crowd does—they see a high P/E and scream "bubble." That's exactly the kind of lazy thinking that lets you miss the next 10-bagger. Remember what I said in *One Up On Wall Street*: **"If you can't find any companies that are attractive, you put your money in the bank until you discover some."** But that doesn't mean you ignore the ones that look scary. You just need to dig deeper.

Let me start with the most important number here: **EPS growth of 362% year-over-year.** That's not a typo. That's a company that's growing like a weed. And what's the P/E? 137. So the PEG ratio—which is my favorite tool for fast growers—is 137 divided by 362. That's a PEG of **0.38**. For the uninitiated, a PEG under 1.0 is undervalued. Under 0.5? That's a gift. I wrote in my book: *"A PEG ratio of 1.0 indicates that a stock's P/E is equal to its earnings growth. That's fair value. PEG under 0.5 is a gift."* (Source: *One Up On Wall Street*, p. 198, Amazon link: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403)

Now, I know what you're thinking: "Pete, that 362% growth rate isn't sustainable." And you're right. It's not. But here's the thing: even if growth decelerates to 50% next year, you're still looking at a PEG of 2.7, which is borderline. And if it decelerates to 30%, you're at 4.6—that's expensive. So the key question is: **Is this a one-trick pony or a sustainable growth story?**

Let's look at the technical picture. The stock is at **$250.81**, which is **17% above its 50-day moving average of $213** and **90% above its 200-day MA of $132**. That's a strong uptrend, and the RSI at 62.4 is not overbought—it's in the healthy zone. Volume is 1.71x average, which tells me institutions are piling in. When I managed Magellan, I loved seeing volume confirm the price action. It means the big money agrees with the story.

Now, the market cap is **$63 billion**. That's not a small-cap fast grower anymore—it's a mid-to-large cap. But here's the thing: I've seen companies like Home Depot and Walmart grow from $10B to $200B market caps and still deliver 20% annual returns. The key is whether the addressable market is huge. If CRDO is in a niche that's growing explosively—like data center connectivity, AI networking, or something similar—then $63B could be just the beginning.

What I want to know next: **Is the company buying back shares?** If management is confident enough to repurchase stock at these prices, that's a huge vote of confidence. **Are insiders buying?** I'd check the SEC filings. **Does the company have net cash?** If cash per share minus debt is significant, that's a margin of safety.

But here's the most important question from my checklist: **Is this a company that any idiot could run?** Because if it is, and it's growing at 362%, then you have a potential 10-bagger. I wrote: *"Go for a business that any idiot can run—because sooner or later, any idiot probably will."* (Source: *One Up On Wall Street*, Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403) If CRDO has a proprietary technology, a strong moat, and a repeatable sales model, then even a mediocre CEO can keep the growth going for years.

The risk here is the **P/E of 137**. If growth slows to 20% next year, that P/E will compress to 68, and the stock could drop 50%. That's the danger of high-growth stocks. But if growth stays above 50%, the stock could double. This is a **high-risk, high-reward** situation.

**My conclusion:** This stock passes the PEG test with flying colors. The technicals are strong. The volume confirms institutional interest. But it's a fast grower, and fast growers are volatile. You need to monitor same-store sales (or whatever their key metric is) every quarter. If that decelerates two quarters in a row, sell without hesitation. But for now, based on the numbers in front of me, I'd say this is a **BUY** for aggressive investors who can stomach the volatility. For conservative investors, wait for a pullback to the 50-day MA around $213.

**Stance: BUY** (with the caveat that you must watch the growth rate like a hawk).
