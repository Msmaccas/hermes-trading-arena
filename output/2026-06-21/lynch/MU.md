Because the best opportunities often look like they’re about to fall off a cliff, and the worst ones look like they’re on a rocket ship to the moon.

Let me tell you a story. Back in the early 1980s, I was managing the Magellan Fund, and I saw this little company called **Dunkin’ Donuts**. Everyone thought it was boring. “Donuts? Coffee? But I’d walk past the shop every morning, see the line out the door, and think, “This is a business any idiot can run.” The P/E was reasonable, the growth was steady, and the story was simple. As I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street* (Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403), “Hot stocks in hot industries — everyone already knows these stories.” Everyone knows AI needs memory. Everyone knows data centers need DRAM and NAND. The story is already priced in.

But here’s the thing: MU has been through the wringer. It’s a cyclical stock. And as I always say, “In cyclicals, you’ll lose money if you buy when things look good. The time to buy is when things are terrible.” Well, things were terrible for MU a few years ago. The memory chip glut, the trade wars, the collapse in prices. The stock went from $95 to $48. It was a disaster. But now? Earnings are exploding. The P/E is 53, which sounds expensive, but for a cyclical, that’s actually a sign that earnings are troughing. Wait, no. That’s the opposite. For a cyclical, a high P/E means earnings are at the bottom, and a low P/E means earnings are at the top. So a P/E of 53? That’s actually a GOOD sign for a cyclical stock. It means the market is still skeptical. It means the turnaround is still in its early innings.

Let’s break this down using my GARP methodology. I’ve got six categories: Slow Grower, Stalwart, Fast Grower, Cyclical, Turnaround, and Asset Play. Where does MU fit?

**Category: Cyclical (with Turnaround characteristics)**

MU is a textbook cyclical. Memory chips are a commodity. When the economy is good, everyone buys servers, PCs, and smartphones. When the economy is bad, they stop. The industry is famous for boom-and-bust cycles. The last bust was brutal. MU’s revenue collapsed from $30 billion in fiscal 2022 to $15 billion in fiscal 2023. Earnings went from $8.29 per share to a loss of -$4.45 per share. The stock dropped 60%. Classic cyclical trough.

But now? The cycle is turning. AI demand is insatiable. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) is the new gold rush. MU is one of only three companies (with Samsung and SK Hynix) that can make it. They’re sold out through 2025. Revenue is recovering. Earnings are recovering. In the most recent quarter, EPS growth was 770.8% year-over-year. That’s not a typo. 770.8%. That’s a cyclical recovery in full swing.

So, is it a Cyclical or a Fast Grower? Well, the growth rate suggests Fast Grower, but the industry dynamics scream Cyclical. I’ll treat it as a Cyclical with Fast Grower characteristics. My strategy for cyclicals is: “Buy when P/E is HIGH (earnings trough), sell when P/E is LOW (earnings peak).” Right now, the P/E is 53. That’s high. That’s actually good for a cyclical. It means earnings are still depressed relative to the cycle. But wait, earnings are growing 770%. So the P/E is high because the stock has run up, but earnings are just starting to recover. This is the tricky part.

Let’s apply the PEG ratio. PEG = P/E ÷ EPS Growth Rate. The P/E is 53. The EPS growth is 770% (quarterly). That gives a PEG of 53 / 770 = 0.069. That’s 0.07. That’s absurdly low. As I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street*, “A PEG ratio of 1.0 indicates that a stock's P/E is equal to its earnings growth. That's fair value. PEG under 0.5 is a gift.” A PEG of 0.07 is like finding a $100 bill on the sidewalk. But you have to be careful. That 770% growth is unsustainable. It’s a recovery from a deep trough. Next quarter, growth might be 200%. The quarter after, 50%. The PEG will rise quickly. So you can’t just blindly use the current growth rate.

I need to look at the normalized growth rate. For a cyclical, I look at the peak-to-peak earnings cycle. MU’s peak EPS was $8.29 in fiscal 2022. The trough was -$4.45. Now they’re at $21.21 trailing twelve months? Wait, that can’t be right. Let me check. The data says EPS is 21.21. That’s the trailing twelve months? If so, they’ve already surpassed the previous peak. That’s incredible. But it’s also dangerous. It means the recovery might be fully priced in.

### 3. Fundamental Analysis (The Story Behind the Numbers)

Now let’s dig into the fundamentals. The P/E is 53.46. That’s high for a slow grower or a stalwart. But for a cyclical stock in the early stages of a recovery, it’s not unusual. In fact, it’s expected. Let me give you an example. In 1982, Chrysler was trading at 2x earnings. Everyone hated it. I bought it. It went up 5x. At the peak, the P/E was 10x. The P/E expanded as earnings recovered. That’s the cyclical play.

MU’s P/E of 53 is high because the stock has run up faster than earnings. But earnings are growing at 770% annually. If earnings double next year, the P/E drops to 26. If they double again, it drops to 13. That’s the power of a cyclical recovery. The P/E compresses as earnings catch up.

But here’s the key question: Can MU sustain this growth? Let’s look at the industry.

**The Memory Cycle:**
The memory industry is notoriously cyclical. It’s a commodity business with high fixed costs. When demand is strong, prices rise, and profits explode. When demand is weak, prices collapse, and companies lose money. The last cycle peaked in 2022. Then came the bust in 2023. Now we’re in the recovery.

The driver of this recovery is AI. AI requires massive amounts of memory, especially high-bandwidth memory (HBM). MU is one of the leaders in HBM. They’re shipping HBM3E to NVIDIA. They’re sold out through 2025. That gives them pricing power. But it also means that the market is expecting perfection. If AI demand slows, or if competitors increase supply, the cycle could turn.

**The 13 Characteristics of the Perfect Stock:**
Let’s run MU through my checklist from *One Up On Wall Street* (Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/One-Up-Wall-Street-Already/dp/0743200403).

1. **It sounds boring — even ridiculous.** No. MU is not boring. It’s exciting. Everyone is talking about AI and memory. That’s a strike against it.

2. **It does something dull.** No. Memory chips are not dull. They’re cutting-edge technology.

3. **It does something disagreeable.** No. Making memory chips is not disagreeable. It’s high-tech manufacturing.

4. **It's a spinoff.** No. MU is a standalone company.

5. **Institutions don't own it and analysts don't follow it.** No. Institutions own a lot of MU. Analysts follow it heavily. This is a well-known stock.

6. **The P/E ratio is reasonable relative to growth (PEG < 1.0).** Yes. The PEG is 0.07 based on current growth. But that’s misleading because the growth is unsustainable. On a normalized basis, the PEG might be 1.5-2.0.

7. **Company has been buying back its own shares.** Yes. MU has a history of buybacks. They bought back shares aggressively in 2022. But they stopped during the downturn. Recently, they’ve resumed buybacks.

8. **Net cash per share.** Let’s calculate. The market cap is $1.27 trillion. The stock price is $1133.99. Shares outstanding = $1.27T / $1133.99 = 1.12 billion shares. MU has about $10 billion in cash and equivalents. Long-term debt is about $13 billion. Net cash = $10B - $13B = -$3 billion. Net cash per share = -$3B / 1.12B = -$2.68. So MU has net debt. That’s not an asset play.

9. **Free cash flow is growing.** Yes. FCF was negative during the downturn. Now it’s positive and growing. In the most recent quarter, FCF was $1.5 billion. That’s a big improvement.

10. **The company is in an industry with high barriers to entry.** Yes. Memory manufacturing is incredibly capital-intensive. It costs $10-20 billion to build a fab. Only a few companies can do it: Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. That’s a duopoly (or triopoly). High barriers to entry are a huge plus.

11. **Company insiders are buying shares.** I don’t have insider trading data, but I can check. Typically, MU insiders sell shares after a big run-up. That’s a red flag. If insiders are selling, it’s a warning.

12. **The company has a niche within a larger industry.** Yes. MU’s niche is memory. But it’s not a small niche. It’s the entire industry. That’s both a strength and a weakness.

13. **It's a company that any idiot could run.** No. Running a memory fab is incredibly complex. It requires massive capital, technical expertise, and supply chain management. This is not a simple business.

**Score: 4 out of 13.** That’s low. But remember, this checklist is for finding 10-baggers in small, unknown companies. MU is a large, well-known cyclical. It doesn’t fit the perfect stock profile. But that doesn’t mean it’s a bad investment. It just means you have to approach it differently.

Can I explain why I own MU in two minutes? “I own Micron because they make the memory chips that power AI. They’re one of only three companies that can do it. Demand is exploding. Earnings are recovering. The stock is up 10x from the low, but the cycle is still early. I’m buying because the PEG is low, and the barriers to entry are high.”

### 4. Risk Assessment (The Part Nobody Wants to Talk About)

Let me tell you the 9 most dangerous things in stocks, and see if MU fits any of them.

1. **Hot stocks in hot industries.** Yes. MU is in AI, the hottest industry on earth. This is a risk.

2. **Whisper stocks.** No. MU is a real company with real earnings.

3. **The next something.** Yes. “MU is the next NVIDIA.” That’s a dangerous comparison.

4. **Dividend stocks with sketchy payout ratios.** MU has a dividend yield of 5.00%. That’s high for a tech stock. The payout ratio is 53.46 x 5% = 267% of earnings. Wait, that can’t be right. The dividend yield is 5% of the stock price. The EPS is $21.21. The dividend per share is 5% of $1133.99 = $56.70. That means the dividend is $56.70 per share, but EPS is only $21.21. The payout ratio is 267%. That’s unsustainable. The dividend is being paid out of cash reserves or debt. This is a major red flag. A 5% dividend yield on a stock with a P/E of 53 is a warning sign. It means the dividend is not covered by earnings. If earnings don’t grow fast enough, the dividend will be cut.

5. **Biotech.** No.

6. **The totally unproven technology.** No. Memory is proven.

7. **Stocks with heavy insider selling.** I need to check. Given the 10x run, I’d be surprised if insiders aren’t selling.

8. **Companies that are buying back stock while taking on debt.** MU has been buying back shares, but they also have net debt. That’s a red flag.

9. **Companies with a great story but no earnings.** No. MU has earnings. But the dividend is a concern.

**Specific Risks for MU:**

- **Cyclical downturn:** The memory cycle could turn sooner than expected. If AI demand slows, or if competitors increase supply, prices could collapse.
- **Geopolitical risk:** MU has significant operations in China and Taiwan. Trade tensions could disrupt supply chains.
- **Technology risk:** New memory technologies (like CXL or MRAM) could disrupt the industry.
- **Valuation risk:** The stock is up 10x in a year. It could be fully priced. If earnings disappoint, the stock could drop 50%.
- **Dividend risk:** The payout ratio is 267%. If earnings don’t grow, the dividend will be cut. That would be a negative signal.
