### 1. Overview & Setup: The "Fast Grower" with a Side of Turnaround Potential

First, let’s categorize Reddit. This is the most important step. You can’t value a cyclical stock the same way you value a stalwart. You can’t value a turnaround the same way you value a slow grower.

Reddit is a **Fast Grower**. Period. End of story. It’s a young company in a massive addressable market (digital advertising). It’s growing revenue at a clip that would make most companies jealous. It’s not a stalwart like Coca-Cola. It’s not a cyclical like Ford. It’s a high-growth, high-risk, potentially high-reward situation.

But here’s the twist. Reddit also has a little bit of **Turnaround** DNA in its blood. For years, Reddit was a mess. Bad management. No monetization strategy. It was the "front page of the internet" that couldn't figure out how to charge for the front page. The IPO was a coming-out party, but it was also an admission that the old way wasn't working. The current management team (Steve Huffman, etc.) is executing a turnaround of the business model. They are finally, *finally*, figuring out how to make money from this massive user base.

So, what caught my eye? **The EPS growth.** Look at this: **EPS Growth (Quarterly): 679.8%.**

Six hundred and seventy-nine percent!

Now, any analyst on Wall Street will tell you that’s a red flag. "Unsustainable!" they'll scream. And they’re right. It is unsustainable. But I’m not looking for sustainability in a fast grower. I’m looking for *acceleration*. I’m looking for the inflection point where the company goes from "burning cash" to "printing cash."

That 679.8% number tells me the company is at an inflection point. They are becoming profitable. They are leveraging their fixed costs. The advertising business is starting to scale. This is the most exciting thing you can see in a fast-growing company.

What worries me? **The P/E ratio.** Look at this: **P/E: 49.98.**

Fifty times earnings. For a company that is just barely profitable.

Let me tell you a story. In the late 1980s, I looked at a company called **Dunkin' Donuts**. I saw the lines out the door every morning. I looked up the company. It was growing earnings at 20% a year. The P/E was 16. The PEG ratio was 0.8. That was a no-brainer. I bought it. It was a multi-bagger.

> *"A company that grows 20% a year with a P/E of 20 is a great investment. A company growing 20% a year with a P/E of 40 is a terrible one."*

If Reddit grows earnings at 50% next year, a P/E of 50 is a bargain. If it grows at 20%, a P/E of 50 is a disaster. That’s the bet you’re making. You’re betting that the growth is real, sustainable, and accelerating.

### 4. Risk Assessment: The 9 Most Dangerous Things

Let’s be honest about the risks. I’ve seen more money lost in "fast growers" than in any other category. The temptation is to fall in love with the story and ignore the risks.

Let me run Reddit through my **9 Most Dangerous Things in Stocks** checklist.

**1. Hot stocks in hot industries?**
Yes. Reddit is a hot stock in the hot AI/social media industry. Everyone knows the story. There are 27 analysts covering it. The "easy" money has been made. This is a risk.

**2. Whisper stocks — the next Microsoft?**
No. Reddit is not a whisper stock. It’s a real company with real revenue. But some people are whispering that it’s "the next Meta." That’s dangerous. It’s not the next Meta. It’s Reddit. It has a different business model.

**3. The next something?**
Yes. "This is the next Twitter." Or "This is the next Facebook." It isn’t. It’s Reddit. It has its own unique DNA. Don’t compare it to other companies. Evaluate it on its own merits.

**4. Dividend stocks with sketchy payout ratios?**

**5. Biotech?**
No. It’s a tech company. But the risk profile is similar. The future is uncertain. The valuation is based on hope.

**6. The totally unproven technology?**
No. The technology is proven. Reddit works. The question is the business model.

**7. Stocks with heavy insider selling?**
This is a critical one. I don’t have the insider trading data in front of me, but I know that after the IPO lockup period expired, there was significant insider selling. That’s a yellow flag. Insiders know the business better than anyone. If they are selling, you have to ask why. Are they diversifying? Or do they know something you don’t?

**8. Companies that are buying back stock while taking on debt?**
No. Reddit has a clean balance sheet. They are not doing financial engineering.

**9. Companies with a great story but no earnings?**
This is the biggest risk. Reddit *just* became profitable. The EPS of $3.50 is real, but it’s fragile. If growth decelerates, the stock will get crushed.

**The Specific Risks for RDDT:**

1. **User Growth Stagnation:** Reddit is not growing users as fast as TikTok or Instagram. If daily active users (DAUs) plateau, the advertising growth story falls apart.
2. **Ad Market Slowdown:** The digital ad market is cyclical. If the economy slows, advertisers cut budgets. Reddit is a "discretionary" ad platform, not a "necessity" like Google Search.
3. **Regulatory Risk:** The EU and other regulators are cracking down on social media. Reddit’s content moderation policies could be a target.
4. **Competition:** Meta is launching "Threads" which is a direct competitor. Google has its own forums. The moat is not as wide as I’d like.
5. **The "WallStreetBets" Factor:** The stock is popular among retail traders on Reddit itself. This creates a cult-like following, but it also creates extreme volatility. The stock can go up 50% on a meme and down 50% on a bad quarter.
