## How I Find Stocks — And Why Qualcomm Fails

I start with 10,000 stocks. Every night, every weekend. I screen for one thing above all else: **earnings acceleration**.

Earnings growth of 25%? That's the MINIMUM. I'm looking for 100-400%. But more importantly, I'm looking for ACCELERATION. EPS up 20% → 50% → 100% over four quarters. That's the pattern I want. That's the pattern of a stock that can double, triple, or quadruple.

Qualcomm is showing the EXACT OPPOSITE.

Revenue growth is NEGATIVE 3.5%. Earnings estimates for fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2027 have declined 8.7% EACH. Over the past 60 days, estimates have dropped 7.3% and 8.4% respectively.

This is not just deceleration. This is a reversal. And when momentum stocks reverse, you GET OUT.

## The Three-Time Champion Rule on Deceleration

Let me be very specific about my rules, because I won three US Investing Championships following them:

**Rule #1: If EPS is Q1:+100% → Q2:+75%, I'm WATCHING.**
**Rule #2: If EPS is Q1:+100% → Q2:+50%, I'm SELLING HALF.**
**Rule #3: If EPS is Q1:+100% → Q2:+25% or REVENUE TURNS NEGATIVE, I'm SELLING EVERYTHING.**

Qualcomm's revenue is NEGATIVE. -3.5%. Not decelerating growth — SHRINKING. The market pays a premium for growth and it compresses multiples when growth disappears. The stock is down 9.6% from its 52-week high and the forward PE has already compressed from the 30s to 21.2. That compression will CONTINUE as estimates keep dropping.

## Why Handset Dependency Makes This Worse

Handsets are MORE THAN 70% of Qualcomm's chip revenue. The smartphone market is mature — you're not going to see 20% unit growth ever again. And analysts now expect global handset units to DECLINE 0-2% year-over-year.

Here's what that means: Qualcomm's biggest market isn't growing. It's SHRINKING. And within that shrinking market, Qualcomm faces Apple's in-house modem development (potential loss of its highest-margin customer), MediaTek competition in mid-range, and the China market contracting 3.3% YoY.

"I only buy the #1 stock in the top industry group." The wireless chip industry group is NOT in the top 10-15. The group is underperforming. And within the group, Qualcomm is losing market share, not gaining it.

## The RS Story

The Relative Strength Rating for Qualcomm would be well BELOW 80 right now. The stock peaked at $250.10, made lower highs, and is now at $226.11. That's a 9.6% decline from the high in a market that has been up 10% year-to-date in the S&P 500.

Think about that: the S&P 500 is up 10% in 2026, and Qualcomm is DOWN 9.6% from its high. That's a Relative Strength disaster. "If a stock can't go up when the market is going up, it will go down twice as fast when the market goes down."

Actually, let me check: Qualcomm is down approximately 22-25% YEAR-TO-DATE. The S&P 500 is up 10%. That means Qualcomm has UNDERPERFORMED the market by 32-35 percentage points this year. The RS line is in freefall.

My rule: sell at RS 80 and below. Qualcomm's RS is probably below 40 by now.

## The Base Pattern — It's Not Forming a Base

When I look at the weekly chart, I see a stock that ran from $124 (52-week low) to $250, got extended, and is now pulling back. The pullback is 9.6% so far. That's not yet a proper base — it's a correction.

But here's what worries me: the stock is ABOVE its 50-day MA by 18.4%. In an uptrend, that's fine. But when a stock is in a correction, being 18% above the 50-day means there's a LOT of room to fall before you even hit support. If the 50-day was rolled forward during the run-up, the current MA doesn't reflect the new reality.

The RSI is 42.3 — bearish, but not oversold. The stock could easily fall to $200 (the 52-week midpoint) before finding real support.

## The Analysts Are Running

Bank of America lowered its rating. Mizuho downgraded. The guidance miss — Q2 revenue guided to $10.2-11.0 billion vs $11.4 billion Street estimate — was a 7-11% miss. That's a BIG miss.

When analysts downgrade en masse, institutions sell. And when institutions sell a $238 billion market cap stock, the selling pressure is sustained, not a one-day event.

## The Verdict

**SELL.** I would not own Qualcomm at any price above $200 based on the current earnings trajectory. The earnings deceleration is unambiguous, the revenue is negative, the industry group is weak, the RS is deteriorating, and the analyst community is running for the exits.

"Three-time champion. The rules don't change." The rules say: when earnings decelerate, sell. When revenue turns negative, sell everything. When RS drops below 80, sell. When the industry group falls out of the top 10, sell.

Qualcomm triggers ALL of these.

The Apple risk is the tail that could wag the dog. Apple's in-house modem development means Qualcomm could lose 10-15% of its revenue from its highest-margin customer in the next 12-24 months. That's a catalyst that the stock has NOT priced in because the timing is uncertain. But the market HATES uncertainty, and the longer Apple's project looms, the more the multiple will compress.

If you own Qualcomm and haven't sold, look at the numbers: -3.5% revenue growth, -8.7% estimate revisions, -22-25% YTD return vs +10% S&P 500. The trade is not working. The stock is not acting right. My rule is simple: "Cut your losses quickly and let your winners run."

Qualcomm is not a winner right now. It's a stock with decelerating earnings in a mature market with a major customer that's building its own alternative. There are 10,000 stocks to choose from — you don't need to own this one.
