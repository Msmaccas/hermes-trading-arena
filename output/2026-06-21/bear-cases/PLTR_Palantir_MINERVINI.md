## VCP in Reverse — Expanding Ranges = Distribution

The Volatility Contraction Pattern works in one direction: each wave of the base shows LESS contraction. Wave 1: 15-30% → Wave 2: 10-20% → Wave 3: 5-10% → Wave 4: 2-5% with drying volume. You buy when the volatility has contracted so tight that the stock is coiled like a spring.

Palantir is doing the OPPOSITE. This is a Volatility EXPANSION pattern:

The stock went from $207 in late 2024 to $160s → bounced to $180s → collapsed through $150 → now $128.47. Each downward wave is WIDER than the last. The range is EXPANDING. Volume on each breakdown is INCREASING. The last wave from $150 to $128 is on 1.2x volume — but that's compared to a 50-day average that already includes elevated selling volume. The actual selling volume at the breakdown was much higher.

"When volatility expands instead of contracts, that's not a setup — that's distribution." The stock is telling you institutions are exiting, not accumulating.

## RSI 23.7 — The False Oversold

A 14-period RSI of 23.7 is technically "oversold." In most textbooks, that's a buy signal. In my experience, "oversold in a downtrend is a trap, not a signal."

It can go from 23.7 to 15 to 10. Buying a stock because RSI is low is like catching a falling knife because you think it's "due to bounce." The knife doesn't care about your RSI reading.

Palantir has been below its 50-day for weeks. The RSI at 23.7 with a failing bounce attempt tells me buyers are exhausted, not that a reversal is imminent.
