## 2. Fundamental Analysis – The Four Gates

### Gate One: Circle of Competence

**The test:** Can you explain in five minutes how this business makes money, who its customers are, and why they stay?

Yes, I can explain it. Reddit makes money by selling advertising on its platform. Its customers are advertisers who want to reach its user base. Its users are mostly young, tech-savvy, and passionate about niche communities.

But here is where the test fails. **Why do they stay?**

I don't invest in what I do not understand. (Source: 2023 CNBC Interview - https://buffett.cnbc.com)

I understand Coca-Cola. I understand why someone buys a Coke. It tastes good. It is everywhere. The brand creates a feeling.

I understand Apple. All I had to do was go out to Nebraska Furniture Mart and talk to customers. (Source: 2026 CNBC Interview - https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-berkshire-hathaway-chairman-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box-today.html)

I do not understand why a Reddit user stays. If a new platform launches tomorrow—say, "BlueSky for Communities"—why wouldn't the users just leave? There is no hardware lock-in. There is no data lock-in. There is no contract. There is just habit. And habit is a weak moat.

I understand the advertising business. I own GEICO. I own See's Candy. But those businesses have something Reddit lacks: a direct relationship with the customer that generates predictable revenue. Reddit's revenue is entirely dependent on "engagement." If engagement drops 10%, revenue drops 10%. That is a fragile business.

**Verdict on Gate One:** **FAIL.** I cannot confidently predict the business 10 years from now.

### Gate Two: Moat Analysis

Let me apply the four types of moats:

| Moat Type | Does RDDT Have It? | Evidence |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Cost Advantage** | NO | Reddit cannot produce or deliver cheaper than Meta, Google, or TikTok. Those companies have massive scale advantages in ad tech and infrastructure. |
| **Network Effect** | WEAK | Reddit has some network effect—more users create more content. But it is a weak network effect because users are not "locked in." They can easily multi-home (use Reddit and other platforms). |
| **High Switching Costs** | NO | What is the cost of leaving Reddit? Zero. You just stop visiting the website. That is the opposite of a switching cost. |
| **Intangible Assets** | NO | Does the "Reddit" brand create pricing power? No. Advertisers buy Reddit because of its audience, not because of the brand. If Reddit raises ad prices 20%, advertisers will simply move to Instagram or TikTok. |

**The moat question:** What keeps competitors out for 10+ years?

I cannot answer that. And if I cannot answer it, I do not invest.

Let me give you an example of a real moat. American Express. They were getting a premium for American Express tickets... they can sell their traveler's check for more than Citigroup, Bank of America, Barclays, everybody had and they were getting a premium at the same time, everybody else was worried about them getting out of business. (Source: 2026 CNBC Interview - https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-berkshire-hathaway-chairman-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box-today.html)

Reddit has no such premium. It is a commodity. And commodities are terrible businesses.

**Verdict on Gate Two:** **FAIL.** No durable competitive advantage.

### Gate Three: Owner Earnings & Intrinsic Value

Let me calculate the **Owner Earnings** for RDDT.

`Owner Earnings = Net Income + Depreciation/Amortization - Maintenance Capital Expenditures`

The reported EPS is $3.50. With 192 million shares outstanding (approximate), that gives us Net Income of roughly $672 million.

But GAAP earnings lie for capital-intensive businesses. Owner Earnings is the truth.

Reddit is not capital-intensive in the traditional sense (no factories, no railroads). But it is "engineering-intensive." It needs to constantly spend on servers, AI moderation, and software engineers to keep the platform running. Let me estimate:

- **Net Income:** $672M
- **Depreciation & Amortization:** Let's assume $150M (typical for tech companies with server infrastructure)
- **Maintenance CapEx:** This is the tricky part. I estimate $200M-$300M per year to maintain the platform (servers, bandwidth, engineering salaries).

**Owner Earnings:** $672M + $150M - $250M = **$572M**

So the business is generating roughly $572 million in true owner earnings per year.

Now, what is the **Intrinsic Value**?

At a $33.68 billion market cap, the market is paying **59x owner earnings**. That is an earnings yield of 1.7%.

Let me compare that to the risk-free rate. The 10-year US Treasury yield is around 4.5%. So the market is saying: "We would rather earn 1.7% on Reddit than 4.5% on a guaranteed government bond."

That makes no sense to me. Unless you believe Reddit can grow its earnings at 20%+ per year for the next decade. And that is a very, very big "unless."

Let me apply the **Retained Earnings Test**. Each dollar retained must create at least one dollar of market value. Reddit has retained all its earnings (no dividend). Has the market value increased by $1 for every $1 retained? In the last year, yes. But over a 5-year horizon? We do not know. And that uncertainty is a red flag.

**Verdict on Gate Three:** **FAIL.** The price implies unrealistic growth expectations.

### Gate Four: The Five-Year Test

> "If the stock market closed for five years tomorrow, would I be happy owning this business based on its cash flow alone?"

If the stock market closed for five years, Reddit would still be operating. It would still be selling ads. But would I be happy owning it?

No. Because I would be earning 1.7% on my money. And that is not enough. Especially when I consider the risk that Reddit's user base could decline, or that a competitor could emerge, or that advertising revenue could shrink in a recession.

I need a margin of safety. At $174.96, there is no margin of safety. There is only hope.

**Verdict on Gate Four:** **FAIL.**

## 4. Risk Assessment – What Could Go Wrong?

Everything. Let me be specific.

**Risk 1: Advertising Recession**
Reddit's revenue is 100% advertising. If the economy slows, advertisers cut budgets. Reddit is a "brand awareness" platform, not a "direct response" platform like Google. In a recession, brand advertising gets cut first. This could crush revenue.

**Risk 2: Competition**
Meta (Facebook/Instagram), Google (YouTube), TikTok, and Snapchat are all fighting for the same ad dollars. Reddit has a unique niche, but that niche is small. If TikTok launches a "community" feature, Reddit could lose users overnight.

**Risk 3: User Churn**
Reddit's user base is passionate but fickle. The "WallStreetBets" crowd could move to a new platform. The "AMA" format could become stale. There is no lock-in.

**Risk 4: Regulatory Risk**
Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act protects platforms from liability for user content. If that changes, Reddit could face massive legal costs. It already struggles with moderation (hate speech, misinformation, etc.).

**Risk 5: Valuation Risk**
At 50x earnings, the stock is priced for perfection. If earnings grow at 20% for 5 years, the P/E would still be high. If growth slows to 10%, the stock could fall 50%.

**Risk 6: Management Risk**
I do not know the management team well. Are they rational capital allocators? Do they buy back stock when it is cheap? Do they make acquisitions that destroy value? I do not have enough information.

**Risk 7: No Moat**
As I said, there is no durable competitive advantage. This is the biggest risk. Without a moat, the business is a commodity. And commodity businesses are terrible investments over the long term.
