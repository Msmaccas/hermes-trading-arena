## MARKET SENTIMENT ASSESSMENT

**Date: 2026-06-08**

Let me cut through the noise. The market told us something important this week, and if you weren’t paying attention to the weekly charts, you got caught flat-footed. The Nasdaq 100 got hammered—down 4.80% in a single week. The S&P 500 dropped 2.58%. The Russell 2000 cratered 3.55%. Semiconductors? Down 9.22%. That’s not a garden-variety pullback. That’s a distribution day parade. And when the leaders get taken out back, you have to ask: Is this just a correction within a bull market, or the beginning of something worse?

I’ve been trading long enough to know that the market top never looks like a top at the time. It always feels like a buying opportunity until it isn’t. But here’s what I see: The QQQ broke down through its 50-day moving average on heavy volume. The weekly chart shows a bearish engulfing candle. The semiconductor index (SMH) lost 9.22% in one week—that’s a cannonball shot across the bow. Tech was the leader, and when the leader stops leading, you need to listen.

Now, look at the sector performance this week. Consumer Staples (XLP) up 1.71%. Utilities (XLU) up 0.93%. Healthcare (XLV) up 0.61%. Real Estate (XLRE) up 0.68%. Defensives are the only spots of green. That’s textbook rotation out of risk assets into safety. In my methodology, I use the weekly chart to find the base and the daily chart to time the entry. But when the weekly charts of the major indices start showing lower highs and higher volume on down weeks, I raise cash. I don’t argue with the market.

Let me quote myself from my 10 Keys to Recognize Market Reversals: *“Learn to recognize major reversal chart patterns—Head & Shoulders, double tops/bottoms, the ‘Frozen Rope’ pattern.”* I see a potential double top forming on the QQQ weekly chart. The index hit an all-time high in late May, then reversed sharply this week on massive volume. That’s textbook distribution. And volume is the fuel that all rallies need to last. When the fuel runs out, you get a frozen rope—a top that looks like a pause but is actually a reversal.

I also look at market leaders. In every rally, there are leaders and laggards. When leaders stop leading, it’s time for caution. This week, the top gainers list is a disaster—penny stocks, micro-caps, Chinese lottery tickets. STI up 57% on no fundamental reason? BNAI up 35%? That’s not leadership. That’s garbage running while quality gets sold. The real leaders like NVIDIA (not in our data but representative of the sector) are getting crushed. Semis down 9% is not a healthy rotation; it’s a flight to safety.

What about the Transportation index? I didn’t get exact data, but the Dow is down 1.35%, and FedEx Freight (FDXF) was actually up 6.42%—a rare bright spot. But one transport stock doesn’t make a trend. The broader Industrials (XLI) were down 1.12%. Defensive behavior.

Now, I know my style: I look for heavy volume breakouts from tight consolidation patterns. But you cannot force trades into a declining market. As I’ve said many times: *“You can’t be afraid to buy a stock at a new high. That’s where the big money is made.”* But this week, very few stocks are making new highs. The ones that are—like ARGX, COO, TXRH—are healthcare and consumer defensive names. That tells you where the money is flowing.

Let me also assess the global picture. China top gainers are mostly micro-cap tech names with absurd percentage moves—20% daily. That’s casino behavior, not institutional accumulation. Japan? Similar story: small cap names like VALUENEX up 19%. Hong Kong? Shandong Molong up 29%. These are speculative froth, not quality breakouts. When the top gainers lists are filled with garbage, the market is in a “sell me your blue chips and buy lottery tickets” phase. That’s a warning.

The Brazil market had some utility and bank names up, but nothing convincing. The UK had Raspberry Pi up 27%—that’s interesting, but it’s a small cap with a PE of 350. Speculative.

So what phase are we in? I’d say we are in Stage 3 of the bull market—late stage with high volatility and distribution days. The market is transitioning from a “buy the dip” to “sell the rip” mentality. My rule: avoid Stage 3 and Stage 4 bases—they fail 80% of the time. We are not in a Stage 4 bear market yet, but the conditions are forming. I need to see the indices hold above their 200-day moving averages. SPY 200-day is around $700 (rough estimate based on 52-week range). SPY closed at $737.55, so we have some room. But one more week like this and that support gets tested.

Volume analysis: The QQQ saw volume above average this week. The SMH volume was likely elevated. In my system, rising volume on down weeks is distribution. Two or three consecutive weeks of distribution signals a market top. This week was the first major distribution week since the April low. I need to see next week’s volume. If we get another down week with increasing volume, I’ll cut all longs and go to cash.

I also watch the 8-period EMA on the daily chart. The QQQ broke below its 8-EMA on Tuesday and never recovered. The 20-EMA is next. If the 20 breaks, then the 50 is vulnerable. That’s when you see panics.

For now, my market sentiment assessment is: **Cautious Bearish**. I am not shorting because that’s not my style—I’m a breakout trader. But I am raising cash, tightening stops, and only looking at the strongest relative strength names that are defying the selling. Those names exist—COO, ARGX, TXRH, FDXF—but they are the exception, not the rule. In a healthy market, you have dozens of high-quality breakouts every week. Right now, I count maybe three or four. That tells me to be selective.

As I’ve said before: *“You can’t force trades. The market is the boss. You either follow it or you lose money.”* So I’m following it. The market is saying “sell tech, buy staples.” I’m listening.
