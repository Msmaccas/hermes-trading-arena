# Matt Caruso — Deep Research Entry
## Week of 2026-06-08

## MARKET SENTIMENT ASSESSMENT

Let me be blunt with you right now: this is not a market where you get to be cute with entries. This is a market that will cut your head off if you size wrong.

Let’s walk through the numbers. The Dow Jones closed at $509.70, down 1.35%. The S&P 500 at $737.55, down 2.58%. The Nasdaq 100 got absolutely hammered at $705.06, down 4.80%. And the Russell 2000? Down 3.55%. That is not a garden-variety pullback. That is a coordinated downdraft across nearly every risk asset class.

But here’s where it gets interesting. You have to look at what’s *inside* those index numbers. Semiconductors (SMH) down 9.22%. Technology (XLK) down 6.66%. ARK Innovation (ARKK) down 6.97%. That is carnage. Meanwhile, Consumer Staples (XLP) up 1.71%. Utilities (XLU) up 0.93%. Healthcare (XLV) up 0.61%. That is a textbook risk-off rotation. Money is leaving high-beta, high-multiple tech and flowing into dividend-paying, recession-resistant sectors. Even gold (GLD) is down 3.65%, which tells you that the sell-off is broad enough to hit even traditional safe havens, probably because the dollar is ripping on the back of that stronger-than-expected jobs report.

Now, what is the driving narrative? The research tells us the trigger was a “stronger-than-expected jobs data” that crushed hopes for monetary policy easing. The market was pricing in rate cuts. The jobs report said: not so fast. And the market repriced accordingly. That is a classic “bad news is good news” reversal — we had gotten spoiled by the idea that the Fed would save us, and suddenly the savior is delayed.

Let me quote myself from the Chat With Traders interview: *“Most traders focus on the wrong thing. They obsess over the perfect entry. I obsess over how much I'll lose if I'm wrong — then I size accordingly.”*

Right now, how much you can lose on a tech position is enormous. ATRs are expanding. Volatility is spiking. That means your position size needs to shrink proportionately. This is non-negotiable. If you try to trade the same size you did two weeks ago, you are going to get blown out.

Let’s talk sector rotation. We saw financials (XLF) flat to slightly positive (+0.21%). That is interesting. Banks benefit from higher rates and a steepening yield curve. But energy (XLE) down 1.84%, materials (XLB) down 1.92% — the cyclical trade is under pressure. Industrials (XLI) down 1.12%. The only green sectors are the defensive ones: staples, utilities, healthcare, real estate. That tells me the market is pricing in a slowdown, not a boom. In fact, the Russell 2000 being down 3.55% is a canary — small caps are more sensitive to the cost of capital, and higher rates choke them.

So where are we in the market phase? I classify markets into four phases: accumulation, trending, distribution, and decline. We are clearly in a decline phase for growth and tech. For defensives, we may be in accumulation or early trending. But the broader market? Distribution that turned into selling climax territory.

The S&P 500 200-day moving average? I don’t have that data point directly, but from the SPY price of $737.55, we can infer it’s probably around $700-720 from prior data. We are still above that, but the 50-day is likely getting tested. The sell-off today was not a crash in a vacuum; it was a high-volume breakdown. That scares me.

But I am not a market timer. I am a position sizer. As I said in the Better System Trader interview: *“Trading is 10% entry, 90% position sizing. You can have the best system, but if you size wrong, you'll go broke.”*

So my sentiment assessment boils down to this: the market is in a risk-off phase. I will not be fighting that. I will be leaning into the stocks that showed relative strength, that have high institutional ownership, that have fundamental catalysts independent of the macro noise. The stocks that went up *today* — COO, ABM, BBCP, ARGX, TXRH, FDXF — those are the ones I want to analyze. Because when the tide goes out, you want to see who is swimming.

We also saw some extreme volume spikes. COO volume 2.94x average. ABM 2.76x. BBCP 11.44x. ARGX 2.31x. That is not random. That is institutions stepping in. They know something. The question is: do we have a setup that aligns with my method?

Let me also note the gold sell-off. GLD down 3.65% to $396.24, below its 50-day MA of $424.89. That is a significant breakdown. Gold had been a darling, but with rates rising, the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset goes up. That is negative for gold in the short term. But long-term, the debt and inflation picture still support it. Not a trade for me right now — too much downside momentum.

Now, let’s talk cash. Remember my rule: *“Max portfolio risk: 6% total at any time. Max risk per trade: 0.5% for normal setups, 1% for high-conviction.”* If I have a $1M account, I can have at most $60,000 total risk across all positions. In this volatility, I wouldn’t even want to use 6%. I’d aim for 3-4% total risk. That means smaller positions, tighter stops, and only the highest quality setups.

The money is not made by being aggressive in a bear phase. The money is made by surviving to trade another day. As I said in the Chigrim interview: *“ATR tells you how much the stock moves. Your position size tells you if you can survive that move.”*

So my overall market call is: **cautious bearish with selective long bias on defensives and high-quality growth that showed strength.** I will be looking for entries on pullbacks, not breakouts. Patience. Cash is a position. Let’s see if this selling exhausts itself.

## FINAL MARKET CALL

Let’s bring it all together. This is a market that is punishing high-beta growth and rewarding defensives. The catalyst is a stronger economy than expected, which pushes rate cuts further out. The result: a repricing of risk assets.

My portfolio positioning for the next two weeks:

**Overall bias:** Cautious bull on defensives and high-quality growth, bear on tech and small caps.

**Cash level:** 80% cash. I will only take 1-2 positions at most. My max portfolio risk is 6%, but I’ll use only 2% total risk across all trades. That means if I take a 0.5% risk trade on COO and another 0.5% on ABM, I’m at 1% risk. That leaves room for error.

**Timeframe:** I am trading on a swing basis, holding positions for days to weeks. I will enter on pullbacks to moving averages. I will scale out at 1R and 2R. I will not add to winners until a confirmed pullback.

**Risk factors:** The biggest risk is a continued sell-off that takes even defensives down. If the S&P 500 breaks below $700 (SPY), all bets are off. I will cut all long positions if the market drops another 5% from here. I also need to watch gold and bonds. TLT fell 0.51% today, but yields are rising. If yields keep rising, utility and consumer staple valuations become less attractive. But for now, the rotation is into them.

**What I am doing:** I have a trigger order to buy COO on a pullback to $64-65. I have a trigger order to buy ABM on a pullback to $39.50-40. I will not chase. If both trigger, I will have about 12-15% of my capital deployed. That leaves me plenty of dry powder.

**What I am NOT doing:** I am not buying ARGX at $891 without a pullback. I am not buying TXRH at $170.46. I am not buying FDXF, NSP, BBCP, or RPI. I am not shorting anything, because shorting in a bear market is crowded and the bounce can kill you. I prefer to wait for a rally to short into.

Let me end with my favorite quote from Van Tharp as taught to me: *“There are no bad trades, only bad position sizes.”* Right now, the biggest mistake you can make is being too large and being forced to sell at the bottom. Use small position sizes. Use wider stops relative to ATR. And wait for the market to tell you when to add.

This is not the time to be a hero. It’s time to be a survivor.
