# Lynch — Deep Research Entry
## Week of 2026-06-08

## MARKET SENTIMENT ASSESSMENT

Here we are, June 8, 2026, and the market just got whacked. Look at the numbers: the Nasdaq fell 4.8%, semiconductors dropped 9.2%, the S&P 500 lost 2.6%, and the Russell 2000 was down 3.6%. Meanwhile, utilities, healthcare, and consumer staples all finished in the green. The Dow only lost 1.35%. That’s a classic flight to safety. People are selling the stuff they thought was going to make them rich quick (tech, AI, semis) and buying the stuff that pays a steady dividend and won’t keep them up at night.

I’ve seen this before. In my book *One Up On Wall Street*, I wrote: “In the stock market, the most important organ is the stomach. It’s not the brain.” (p. 24). Right now, investors’ stomachs are churning. The trigger? A stronger-than-expected jobs report. That means the Fed isn’t going to cut rates anytime soon. And when the Fed stays tight, high-growth stocks get crushed because their future earnings are worth less today. That’s exactly what hit the semis and the tech-heavy Nasdaq.

But here’s the thing: this sell-off is a gift for patient investors who know what they own. I tell my Fidelity analysts all the time: “Your biggest winners will go up and down until they go up 10x. If you sell too early, you miss the real move.” The key is to separate the noise from the story. A jobs report doesn’t change the fact that a company like Texas Roadhouse is still selling more steaks, or that argenx is still getting more patients on VYVGART. If the story is intact, a 5% or 10% drop is just a buying opportunity.

Now, let’s look at the sector rotation in detail. Consumer staples (XLP) up 1.7%. Utilities (XLU) up 0.9%. Healthcare (XLV) up 0.6%. These are your stalwarts and slow growers. They don’t grow fast, but they don’t fall apart in a sell-off either. I often say: “Stalwarts are the stocks you buy when you don’t want to lose money. You won’t get rich, but you won’t get poor.” That’s what people are doing right now. Meanwhile, the tech sector (XLK) lost 6.7%, consumer discretionary (XLY) lost 2%. People are rotating out of the high-risk stuff.

But here’s where most investors get it wrong. They see tech falling and think the whole market is doomed. In reality, the rotation tells you something different: there’s still money in stocks, it’s just moving. The market is not crashing; it’s rebalancing. I wrote in *One Up On Wall Street*: “The market is a voting machine in the short term and a weighing machine in the long term.” (p. 36). Right now, the voting machine is voting for safety. But the weighing machine—the actual value of companies—hasn’t changed just because the jobs number came in strong.

What about the bear market fear? We’re not in a bear market. The S&P 500 is only about 10% off its all-time high. And the Dow is down just a little. But tell that to someone who owns SMH (semiconductors) down 9% in one day. That hurts. That’s why I always remind myself: “The real key to making money in stocks is not to get scared out of them.” If you own a good company at a reasonable price, you ride through these dips. You don’t sell Ford because the currency market goes crazy for a week.

Now, let’s look at the international markets for clues. The top gainers in China are all small-cap tech names—robotics, digital twin, 3D sensing. That tells me there’s still speculative money chasing high-growth stories outside the US. In Hong Kong, Shandong Molong (+29%) is a petroleum machinery company. That’s a cyclical. And cyclicals at the top of the list often mean people are betting on a recovery in oil and gas. In India, the top movers are micro-cap finance and logistics companies—again, small cap speculation. In the UK, Raspberry Pi is up 27% on a single day. That’s a low-float, high-volatility tech stock. And Tate & Lyle surged 16% on takeover rumors. That’s a classic asset play, and I’ll talk about that later.

So what’s the market phase? I’d say we’re in a correction within a longer-term uptrend. The economy is still hiring (strong jobs number), corporate earnings are generally solid (Texas Roadhouse, ABM, argenx all reported well), but the market is digesting the higher-for-longer interest rate reality. The sectors that led—AI chips, mega-cap tech—are now giving back some gains. That’s healthy. A market that never goes down is a bubble. A market that corrects 10% every so often is a normal market.

My biggest concern? Semiconductors down 9% in one day. That’s a serious shellacking. If you own a cyclical stock in a downturn, you can lose 50% in a few months. I know because I’ve lived it. In my book I said: “Cyclical stocks are the most dangerous stocks to own. If you buy a cyclical at the wrong time, you can lose 50% in a few months.” That’s the lesson. People who bought SMH today are getting that lesson in real time. But for patient long-term investors, this creates opportunities in high-quality growth stocks that are now on sale.

Now, let’s talk about the individual stocks on my radar. I’ve got a mix of fast growers, asset plays, turnarounds, and stalwarts. Each one needs to be judged by its own story, not by what the market did yesterday.

But wait—forward PE is only 22.29, against a forward EPS of $7.65. If you use forward growth, maybe it’s 22.29/12.8 = 1.74. Still high, but not insane.

But I don’t buy stocks based solely on PEG. I buy based on the story and the numbers together. Texas Roadhouse has an ROE of 28.9%—that’s excellent. Profit margin 6.8% is good for restaurants. Free cash flow of $190 million is strong. Debt/equity at 68% is manageable. And they pay a dividend with a 44.5% payout ratio, which is sustainable.

People who are tired of Chipotle and McDonald’s go there for a real steak. They keep coming back. Each new store adds to earnings. That’s it. A ten-year-old gets it.

Now, the sell rule for a fast grower: “Sell when the growth rate starts to slow.” Right now, growth is still strong. Same-store sales are positive. The 52-week range is $153 to $197, so we’re closer to the low end. The analyst target is $197, implying 16% upside. I think it could go higher. But I also note that short interest is 5.63%, which is elevated. That means some people are betting against it. In a market rotation away from consumer cyclicals, that could be a risk. But today’s price action suggests the shorts are losing.

My entry level: current price $170 is fine. I’d buy more on dips to $160, which is near the 50-day moving average. For exit, I’d hold until the story changes—if store openings slow, if margins compress, or if the PEG goes above 2.5. Until then, let it ride.

## FINAL MARKET CALL

So what do I do with my portfolio? Let’s step back. The market is in a correction, led lower by tech and semiconductors. The rotation into defense is real. But I don’t see a recession—jobs are strong, and companies like Texas Roadhouse and ABM are reporting good results. The sell-off is about interest rates, not about earnings.

My overall positioning: I’m 60% long, 40% cash. I’m not selling my winners. I’m adding to my best ideas on weakness. The cash gives me the ability to buy if things get cheaper. I’ve learned from experience: “If you sell a stock because it goes down, you’re doing it wrong. You sell because the story changes.”

Right now, the stories are intact for the stocks I like. Texas Roadhouse is still selling steaks. ABM is still cleaning buildings. argenx is still selling VYVGART. The only change is the price. And I love buying quality on sale.

Cash level: I recommend 25-40% cash for most investors. That way you don’t panic when the market drops another 5%.

Timeframe: This is a 12-24 month hold. The rotation from growth to value could last a while. Healthcare and consumer staples could be the winners of the next year. I’m buying ABM and TXRH for the long term. I’m small in NSP as a turnaround speculation. And I’m watching COO for a better entry.

Risk factors: The biggest risk is that the Fed tightens too much and causes a recession. If unemployment starts rising, cyclicals will get hit hard. I’d then cut my exposure to TXRH and FDXF. The second risk is that the AI bubble fully pops, dragging down all stocks. But I’m not in AI stocks, so I’m somewhat insulated.

Remember what I always say: “In the stock market, the most important organ is the stomach. It’s not the brain.” Stay calm, stick to your story, and don’t let a bad week change your investment thesis. The market will recover. It always does. And the best time to buy was yesterday, but the second best is today. I’m buying quality at reasonable prices.

Good luck, and keep looking for those tenbaggers. They’re out there. You just have to know what to look for.
