## MARKET SENTIMENT ASSESSMENT

Let me tell you something right now — this market is sending mixed signals, and that's exactly when most traders get killed. If the market is telling you it's weak, listen."

Let me walk through each major market one by one.

**Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) — $705.06, down 4.80%**

This is the story this week. A 4.8% single-day decline in the QQQ is not a normal pullback. That's distribution. That's institutional selling. When I see the semiconductor index (SMH) down 9.22% — I've seen this movie before. Semiconductors lead both on the way up and on the way down. The SMH has corrected over 9% in a single session. That's not a garden-variety dip; that's a warning shot across the bow.

The news on Friday was a strong jobs report, which pushed yields higher. That's what triggered the tech selloff. Higher yields = lower present value of future earnings, and tech stocks are all about future earnings. This is textbook.

But here's the thing — I don't trade on "reasons." I trade on price and volume. And the price action in the QQQ and SMH is telling me to be extremely cautious.

**S&P 500 (SPY) — $737.55, down 2.58%**

These are defensive rotations. Money is moving out of growth and into safety. When institutional money starts rotating like this, you need to pay attention. It's not a crash scenario — yet — but it's a clear signal that the risk-on trade is being unwound.

As I said in *Mindset, Methods, and Market Strategies*, "When the market is in a confirmed uptrend, be fully invested. When it's not, sit on your hands. It's that simple — but the hardest thing to do."

Right now, I would NOT call this a confirmed uptrend. We need to see what happens next week. If the selling continues and we break below key moving averages, we're in a correction.

**Russell 2000 (IWM) — $281.65, down 3.55%**

Small caps getting hit harder than the S&P 500 but less than the Nasdaq. That's consistent with a risk-off move. Small caps are more sensitive to economic conditions, and a strong jobs report means the Fed stays hawkish. That's bad for small caps that rely on cheap debt.

**Dow Jones (DIA) — $509.70, down 1.35%**

The Dow held up best, down only 1.35%. That's the "old money" index. Utilities, staples, healthcare, financials — these are all Dow components or Dow-like sectors. The Dow is saying "this is a tech rotation, not a crash."
