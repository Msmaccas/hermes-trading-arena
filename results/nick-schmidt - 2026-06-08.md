# Nick Schmidt — Deep Research Entry
## Week of 2026-06-08

## MARKET SENTIMENT ASSESSMENT

**Date:** 2026-06-08  
**Market Phase:** Distribution / Correction — Risk-Off Rotation in Full Effect

Let me start with what’s obvious to anyone who’s watched the price action: the major indices are bleeding, and the leadership that carried this market for the past year is getting pummeled. The Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) closed at $705.06, down 4.80% on the day. The semiconductors (SMH) got hammered for 9.22%. ARK Innovation (ARKK) fell 6.97%. Technology (XLK) lost 6.66%. Those are not normal day-to-day moves—they’re distribution days with institutional volume.

Meanwhile, the defensive sectors flickered green: Consumer Staples (XLP) +1.71%, Utilities (XLU) +0.93%, Healthcare (XLV) +0.61%, Real Estate (XLRE) +0.68%. Even Financials (XLF) eked out a +0.21% gain. That’s textbook rotation out of risk and into safety. The S&P 500 (SPY) dropped 2.58%, but the Russell 2000 (IWM) fell 3.55%—small caps getting crushed even harder.

What’s driving this? The news flow points to a stronger-than-expected jobs report that doused hopes for near-term monetary easing. When the market was pricing in rate cuts and got a reality check, the hottest names got sold first. Semiconductors are the canary in the coal mine here—when SMH loses 9%, it’s not a pullback, it’s a rout.

Now, how does this fit into my methodology? I’ve been here before. I wrote about these moments in *Trading Mindfully* and discussed them in countless chartpattern.com newsletters. The first rule of survival in a market like this is: **Don’t confuse a macroeconomic headwind with a stock-specific setup.** The market is always teaching, and right now it’s teaching humility.

> “In 2014 my average gain was 9%, my average loss was 6%, my average holding time was two days, and I made over 600 trades. I was working my butt off to just lose money.”  
> — Nick Schmidt, TraderLion Webinar

I share that because this week’s action is precisely the kind of environment where a trader can blow up trying to be a hero. In 2014, I would have been scalping these breakouts and getting stopped out repeatedly because the market wasn’t cooperating. The quote reminds me—and should remind you—that the difference between winning and losing is often knowing when to sit on your hands.

> “I think most people's errors and what's holding them back is from them over-trading and doing things when they should not be doing anything at all.”  
> — Nick Schmidt, TraderLion Webinar

That’s the core message right now. The market is in a correction phase. Until we see a follow-through day or a cluster of high-volume accumulation days, the odds favor further downside. The only stocks that should interest us are those with such strong institutional sponsorship and tight technical patterns that they can decouple from the broader market—and even then, position size should be halved.

Let me break down the sectors:

- **Semiconductors (SMH -9.22%):** The epicenter of the pain. A 9% drop in a sector that led the rally means the leadership is cracking. I need to see that sector stabilize before I even consider a growth stock position that relies on cyclical demand.
- **Technology (XLK -6.66%):** Broad tech sell-off. Mega-cap names are getting hit. This is not the time to average down or add to a position just because it’s “on sale.” Price is the ultimate indicator.
- **Consumer Discretionary (XLY -2.05%):** Less severe, but still negative. Inside that index, Texas Roadhouse (TXRH) was up 5.68%—more on that later. But the sector is still backward.
- **Healthcare (XLV +0.61%):** One of the few bright spots. Biotech (IBB) was down 1.74%, but argenx (ARGX) was up 5.82%. This is where relative strength lives.
- **Defensives (XLP, XLU, XLRE):** All positive. The market is paying for safety. This tells me the broad appetite for risk is gone.

Now, the most important question: **What phase of the market cycle are we in?** Using my weekly chart framework, I look for the interaction of price with the 10-week and 30-week SMAs. On the S&P 500, the SPY closed at $737.55. I don’t have the weekly close yet (it’s Monday morning), but Friday’s action likely took the index below its 10-week SMA and maybe even the 30-week. That’s a broken trend from a short-term perspective. We’re in a correction that could deepen into a bear market if the selling continues with volume.

> “When you have a stock that comes off lows, has a massive trend, and then builds such a controlled base near highs — that's telling you it's being accumulated within this base.”  
> — Nick Schmidt, TraderLion Webinar

The problem is, the market as a whole is not building a controlled base. It’s breaking down from a high level. That’s distribution, not accumulation. Until we see a base form and hold above the 30-week SMA, I’m treating this as a risk-off environment.

> “I value time not money. I like money because it usually gives you time. But I value time over it.”  
> — Nick Schmidt, TraderLion Webinar

This is where the psychology kicks in. I don’t feel compelled to trade because there’s a movement. I value my time more than the potential 10% gain on a risky dip-buy. The market is offering few high-probability setups. So I’m going to be patient, sit in cash for the most part, and only consider the strongest names with verified institutional sponsorship.

Now let’s walk through each stock on the watchlist. I’ll apply the same standard I use every week: weekly chart, controlled base near highs, volume confirmation, and a clear risk management plan. Not every stock that made the gainers list belongs in your portfolio.
