## MARKET SENTIMENT ASSESSMENT

Let’s cut through the noise. The market is speaking, and it’s speaking in distribution days. Look at the numbers from June 5, 2026: Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) down 4.80% on heavy volume. Semiconductors (SMH) crushed 9.22%. Technology (XLK) down 6.66%. The Russell 2000 (IWM) dropped 3.55%. The S&P 500 (SPY) lost 2.58%. And what did the defensive sectors do? Consumer Staples (XLP) up 1.71%. Utilities (XLU) up 0.93%. Healthcare (XLV) up 0.61%. This is not a normal pullback. This is institutional selling—distribution day after distribution day.

**Distribution days are the market's way of telling you the professionals are getting out.** And right now, those professionals are dumping tech and rotating into safety. The Nasdaq alone has been hit by multiple distribution days in the last four to five weeks. We’re looking at a cluster of five, six, maybe more. That’s the market’s warning signal. As I wrote in *How to Make Money in Stocks* (p.210): “A cluster of 5-6 in a few weeks means get defensive.” That’s where we are.

Let’s walk through every major index. The Dow Jones (DIA) fell 1.35%. Not as bad, but still a loss on heavy volume? We don’t have volume for each day, but the overall pattern is clear: breadth is breaking down. The Russell 2000 was hit harder than the Dow. Small caps are supposed to lead in a healthy bull market. When they get hammered like this, it tells you the “animal spirits” are gone. The market is contracting.

Gold (GLD) fell 3.65%—that’s interesting. In a flight to safety, gold usually rallies. But here it’s down, which suggests a liquidity event. Maybe margin calls are forcing selling of everything. That’s bearish.

Sector rotation is screaming “risk off.” Only five sectors were positive: Consumer Staples, Utilities, Real Estate, Healthcare, and Financials (barely +0.21%). Financials are a mixed signal—they can lead in rising rate environments, but the rest of the market is bleeding. The old leadership—technology, semiconductors, consumer discretionary (XLY down 2.05%), ARK Innovation (ARKK down 6.97%)—is being decimated. This is a classic topping process.

**The public is always wrong about the market at major turning points. CANSLIM keeps you on the right side.** What did the public believe just a few weeks ago? That AI and tech could never go down. They were piling into SMH, XLK, QQQ at the highs. Now they’re trapped. The professionals—the institutions—are the ones distributing shares. They don’t sell into bad news; they sell into strength or on quiet days. But here we had a strong jobs report on June 5, and the market sold off hard. That’s a textbook sign: good news is ignored, bad news is amplified. The market is fading the rally attempts.

What about the attempted rally? We had a bounce attempt earlier this week? The data shows Friday (June 5) was a big down day. The week prior? We need to count distribution days. I don’t have the daily bars, but the cumulative evidence is overwhelming. Look at the 50-day moving averages: SMH at $569.69—probably below its 50-day. QQQ at $705.06—likely below its 50-day. The S&P 500 SPY at $737.55—check its 50-day? Not provided, but the 200-day average? Probably still above? But that doesn’t matter. What matters is the pattern of distribution. You can’t buy into this.

**The big money is not in the buying and selling, but in the waiting.** The best thing you can do right now is raise cash. In CANSLIM, the M is the most important letter. Most investors lose money not because they buy bad stocks, but because they buy good stocks in a bad market. How many times have I seen this? The investor finds a stock with great earnings, a beautiful cup-with-handle, and then buys it the day before the market rolls over. That stock goes down with everything else. The loss is cut at 7-8%, but it’s still a loss. And if you don’t cut, it becomes a 20% loss, then 40%. That’s how fortunes are destroyed.

We need a follow-through day to confirm a new uptrend. Right now, we don’t have one. From *How to Make Money in Stocks* (p.217): “The follow-through day is the most important concept in market timing. Do not buy before it.” We are in an attempted rally count? Probably day 1 or 2 of a new follow-through attempt if we had a down day on Friday. But the market is still in a downtrend. The 50-day moving averages are rolling over. The relative strength lines of the leading stocks are breaking.
